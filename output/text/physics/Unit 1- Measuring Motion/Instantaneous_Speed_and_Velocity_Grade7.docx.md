Instantaneous Speed and Velocity - Grade 7 Physics
🚗 What Is Instantaneous Speed?
• Instantaneous speed is the speed of an object at a specific moment in time.
• It’s what a speedometer in a car shows—your speed right now.
Example:
If you're jogging and look at your smartwatch, it might say:
- “Speed: 2.4 m/s”
That’s your instantaneous speed—how fast you’re going at that exact second.
📍 What Is Instantaneous Velocity?
• Just like instantaneous speed, but it includes direction.
• So if you're running at 2.4 m/s north, that’s your instantaneous velocity.
• Velocity = speed + direction.
📈 How Is It Different from Average Speed?
🧪 Example Activity (Classroom or Home)
The Stopwatch Drill:
- Use a stop clock or mobile timer.
- Run a short 10-meter distance.
- Have a friend time you at:
• The start
• The middle
• The end
- Record your speed at each point by calculating:
Speed = Small distance ÷ Time between observations
You’ll notice your instantaneous speed may change during your run.
🎯 Key Takeaways
• Instantaneous speed is how fast you're going right now.
• Instantaneous velocity tells you how fast and in what direction, at that moment.
• It’s not the same as average speed, which looks at the whole trip.
