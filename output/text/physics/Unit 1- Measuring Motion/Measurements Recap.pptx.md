What is Measurement?
Measurement is how we find out how much, how long, or how heavy something is—by comparing it to a known standard (like a meter, kilogram, or second).

Accuracy, Precision and Significant Figures - Recap from Grade 6
Least Count
Least Count is the smallest length, mass, time, or volume that a measuring tool can read accurately.
Eg: Measuring Cylinder:
If the smallest graduation is 2 mL:Least count = 2 mLIf the water level is between 24 and 26 mL, you can't say it's 25.2 mL with confidence.
Tip: Always record values up to the least count — don’t guess beyond it!
Eg: Weighing Scale (Digital)
If a kitchen scale shows mass like 125.0 g, 125.5 g, 126.0 g —Least count = 0.5 gSo, you shouldn’t write 125.36 g unless the scale can show that.
Why Does Least Count Matter?
It tells us how precise our measurements are.
It prevents us from recording values we can’t actually see.
If two students use instruments with different least counts, one will get more precise results.
Eg: If a ruler has markings for every 1 mm, then:Least count = 1 mmThis means the ruler can measure lengths as small as 1 mm.
Ask: Can this ruler measure something that is 0.25 mm long?No — that's smaller than the least count!

Significant digits (also called significant figures or “sig figs” for short) indicate the precision of a measurement.  A number with more significant digits is more precise.  For example, 8.1 cm is more precise than 8 cm. It’s sometimes easier to think of this in terms of recording all of the known “places” (ones, tenths, hundredths) plus a final estimated place.
