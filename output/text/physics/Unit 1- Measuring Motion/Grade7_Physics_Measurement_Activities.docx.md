Grade 7 Physics: Measurement & Calculation Activities
Activity 1: Treasure Chest of Quantities
Objective: Brush up understanding of fundamental vs derived quantities, SI units, and unit conversions.
Time: 15–20 minutes
Materials: Worksheet, ruler (optional)
Worksheet
Part A – Sort the Quantities
Instructions: Categorize the following quantities as Fundamental (F) or Derived (D).
1. Mass
2. Volume
3. Time
4. Speed
5. Force
6. Temperature
7. Area
8. Pressure
9. Length
10. Density
11. Current
12. Acceleration
Part B – Fix the Unit!
Instructions: Circle the correct SI unit for each quantity.
1. Force – (a) N (b) kg (c) Pa
2. Pressure – (a) N (b) m/s² (c) Pa
3. Volume – (a) m³ (b) cm (c) kg
4. Time – (a) s (b) hr (c) min
5. Temperature – (a) °F (b) K (c) C
Part C – Convert the Units
Instructions: Show the correct conversion and answer.
1. 2.5 km = ______ m
2. 300 cm³ = ______ L
3. 5.2 hr = ______ s
4. 120 g = ______ kg
Activity 2: Mystery Object Measurements
Objective: Calculate area, volume, and density for regular and irregular shaped objects.
Time: 15–20 minutes
Materials: Worksheet, ruler, measuring cylinder, string, eraser, stone, marble
Worksheet
Part A – Regular Object (Eraser)
1. Measure length, breadth, and height (in cm):
Length: _____ cm
Breadth: _____ cm
Height: _____ cm
2. Calculate Area of top face: ________________
3. Calculate Volume: __________________
4. Given Mass: 25 g
Density = Mass / Volume = _______________
Part B – Irregular Object (Stone/Marble)
1. Mass: ______ g
2. Volume by water displacement: ______ cm³
3. Density = Mass / Volume = _______________
4. Which method was more accurate and why? ___________________________
Activity 3: How Good is Your Measurement?
Objective: Understand precision, accuracy, significant figures, and least count.
Time: 15–20 minutes
Materials: Worksheet, ruler, sample measurements
Worksheet
Part A – Least Count Match
Match the instrument to its least count:
1. Ruler – (a) 0.1 cm
2. Stopwatch – (b) 1 s
3. Thermometer – (c) 0.5°C
4. Digital Scale – (d) 1 g
Part B – Measuring for Sig Figs
Three students measured the length of a pen:
A: 14.0 cm
B: 14.01 cm
C: 13.8 cm
1. Who is most precise? _______
2. Who is most accurate? (Assume actual = 14.00 cm) _______
3. How many significant figures in each measurement?
Part C – Error Discussion
Which of the following is a random error?
(a) Student misreading scale
(b) Parallax error
(c) Faulty stopwatch
(d) All of the above
