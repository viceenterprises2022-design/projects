🧪 Experiment Title:
Investigating the Relationship Between Force, Mass, and Acceleration
Aim:
To determine how the acceleration of an object depends on the force applied and its mass.
📊 Sample Data (Keeping Mass Constant, Varying Force)
Research Question (What is the question you are investigating?):
Hypothesis:
Observation: As the applied force increases, acceleration increases proportionally when mass is constant.
📊 Sample Data (Keeping Force Constant, Varying Mass)
RQ(What is the question you are investigating?):
Hypothesis:
Observation: As the mass increases, acceleration decreases for the same applied force.
🧭 Criterion C: Processing and Evaluating
C i — Presenting and Transforming Data
Plot Graph 1: Force (N) on the x-axis and Acceleration (m/s²) on the y-axis for the first dataset (mass constant).
Describe the shape of the graph and what it indicates.
Plot Graph 2: Mass (kg) on the x-axis and Acceleration (m/s²) on the y-axis for the second dataset (force constant).
Describe the trend observed.
Use your data to calculate the ratio F/a for each trial in Graph 1. What do you notice about the value? What does this represent?
For Graph 2, calculate ( F = m X a ) to verify whether your measured values satisfy Newton’s Second Law.
C ii — Interpreting Data and Drawing Conclusions
Based on Graph 1, describe how acceleration changes with applied force when mass is constant.
(Is the relationship direct, inverse, or nonlinear?)
Based on Graph 2, describe how acceleration changes with mass when force is constant.
(Does it increase, decrease, or remain the same?)
Combine your findings to state the mathematical relationship among F, m, and a.
(Write the formula and explain in words what it means.)
Does your data support the hypothesis that acceleration is directly proportional to force and inversely proportional to mass? Justify with one data example from each table.
C iii — Evaluating Validity and Reliability
Identify one systematic error and one random error that could have affected your results.
Example: friction on the trolley track, delay in timing measurements, inconsistent force application.
Suggest two improvements to make your data more reliable or accurate.
Example: use smoother track, repeat trials, use a digital motion sensor instead of manual timing.
If the experiment were conducted on the Moon, where gravity is weaker, would your force–mass–acceleration relationship change? Explain why or why not.
