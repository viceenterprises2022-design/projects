Gold, God, and Glory - The Age of Exploration
Introduction to the Three G’s
Historians often sum up the main reasons Europeans began exploring the world from about
1400 to 1750 with the phrase “Gold, God, and Glory.” This was a time when Europeans set
out on long sea voyages, discovered new lands, and built overseas empires — a period called
the Age of Exploration.
1) Gold - Wealth and Trade
One of the strongest motivations was wealth. Europeans wanted:
●  Gold and silver from new lands.
●  Rich trade goods like spices, silk, and other valuable products from Asia and the
Americas.
●  To find direct sea routes to Asia so they wouldn’t have to depend on middlemen
traders.
Merchants convinced kings and queens to fund voyages. If explorers found new trade routes or
treasures, both the rulers and the merchants stood to gain great profits.
2) God - Religion and Conversion
Religion was another key motive. Many Europeans believed it was their duty to spread
Christianity to people they met in distant lands. They wanted to:
●  Convert non-Christian peoples to Christianity.
●  Compete with other religions like Islam.
This mission of spreading religion gave explorers and countries a strong purpose, and in many
places they built missions and churches.
3) Glory - Power, Fame, and Competition
The third reason was glory - meaning:
●  Fame and honour for the explorers themselves.
●  Prestige and power for their countries.
●
Intense competition between European rulers to claim new lands before their rivals.
Kings and queens believed that the more territory they controlled overseas, the stronger and
more respected their nation would be.

The Age of Exploration changed the world in many ways:

In Simple Terms
●  Gold = Wealth and valuable goods.
●  God = Spreading religion.
●  Glory = Fame and power for individuals and countries.
Together, these motives explain why Europeans explored, conquered, and connected
distant parts of the world, shaping global history during the early modern era.
