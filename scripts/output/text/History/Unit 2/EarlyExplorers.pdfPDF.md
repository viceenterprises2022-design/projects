Early Explorers
1. Marco Polo (1254–1324) – Land Explorer
●  Venetian merchant who traveled along the Silk Road with his father and uncle.
●  Reached Kublai Khan’s court in China and observed Asian cities, cultures,
technologies, and trade.
●  Spent almost 17 years in the Mongol Empire.
●  His book The Travels of Marco Polo amazed Europeans with descriptions of China’s
wealth (paper money, coal, silk).
●  Some Europeans doubted his stories, but the book increased curiosity, trade interest,
and exploration.
●  Significance: Inspired future explorers like Christopher Columbus.
2. Zheng He (1371–1433) – Chinese Maritime Explorer
●  Born Ma He; became a trusted admiral of the Ming dynasty.
●  Led seven voyages between 1405–1433 with massive “treasure ships”.
●  Reached places like Malacca, India, Sri Lanka, Arabia, Somalia, and Kenya.
●  His voyages helped spread Chinese goods such as silk and porcelain, and brought back
exotic animals (e.g., giraffes) to China.
●  Strengthened China’s tributary system, where foreign rulers acknowledged the Chinese
emperor.
●  After his death, China stopped its long-distance voyages.
●  Significance: Showed China’s naval strength long before European exploration.
3. Bartolomeu Dias (c.1450–1500) – Portuguese Navigator
●  First European to sail around the Cape of Good Hope in 1488.
●  His journey proved that the Atlantic Ocean connected to the Indian Ocean, making
new trade routes possible.
●  Faced storms, food shortages, and dangerous seas.
●  Although he turned back before reaching India, his route gave Portugal confidence to
continue.
●  Significance: Laid the foundation for Vasco da Gama’s expedition.
4. Vasco da Gama (c.1460–1524) – Reaching India by Sea
●  Completed the first direct sea voyage from Europe to India in 1498.
●  Used a local Indian pilot from East Africa who guided him to Calicut.
●  Brought back spices like pepper and cinnamon, making Portugal extremely rich.
●  His later voyages helped establish Portuguese forts and trading posts along the Indian
Ocean coast.
●  Significance: Started centuries of European presence and control in the Indian Ocean.
