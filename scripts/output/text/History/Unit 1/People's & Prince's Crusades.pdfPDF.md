First Crusade Preparations
●  Pope Urban II called for the First Crusade in 1095.
●  Many people in Europe, including nobles (“princes”) and ordinary people
(“peasants” etc.), were inspired to go.
●  People who took the Cross (promised to go) needed to raise money (selling
or mortgaging land) for equipment, travel etc.
People’s Crusade
Who led them / Composition
●  Led by popular preachers, especially Peter the Hermit and Walter
Sansavoir.
●  Mostly ordinary people: peasants, some knights, but poorly organized.
What happened
●  They left earlier than the Princes’ armies, without waiting for the main force.
●  As they traveled, they caused disorder in places: e.g. Hungary, Bulgaria.
●  They arrived at Constantinople; the Byzantine emperor Alexius advised
them to wait.
●  They crossed the Bosporus (on 6 August 1096) and moved further.
●  They were ambushed by the Turks at a place called Cibotus or “Civetot” and
almost entirely destroyed.
Other effects
●  Some groups among the People’s Crusade committed massacres (pogroms)
of Jews in some German/Rhenish towns (e.g. Speyer, Worms, Mainz,
Cologne).
●  Many of these groups never made it near Jerusalem.
Prince’s Crusade (Main Crusading Armies, sometimes called the “Princes’
Crusade”)
Who
●  Led by nobles/princes (i.e. high-ranking leaders) rather than by the people or
peasants. Examples:
• Godfrey of Bouillon (from Lower Lorraine)
• Raymond of Saint-Gilles (Count of Toulouse)
• Bohemond of Taranto
When / Movement
●  They set out in August 1096 following the People’s Crusade. (Encyclopedia
Britannica)
●  They travelled through Europe, crossed Byzantine territory, then the
Bosporus into the Near East.
Relations with others
●  When they reached Constantinople, Emperor Alexius I had concerns: large
foreign armies entering his realm, questions about loyalty.
●  The Crusader princes had to swear oaths to Alexius: promise to return any
lands they recaptured that had once belonged to the Byzantine Empire and to
respect his frontier while passing through his territories.
●  Not all were enthusiastic about these oaths; some did so under pressure.
Size / Equipment / Logistics
●  Much better organized than People’s Crusade. They had knights,
men‐at‐arms, infantry; they carried equipment; had supply lines etc.
●  They had to arrange provisions and markets in Byzantine lands, cross seas,
avoid or manage conflicts with local rulers (including the Byzantines).
