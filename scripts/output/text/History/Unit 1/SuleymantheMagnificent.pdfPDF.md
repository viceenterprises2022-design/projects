Suleyman the Magnificent
1. Quick facts
●  Born: November 1494 – April 1495.
●  Died: September 5/6, 1566, near Szigetvár, Hungary.
●  Sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1520 to 1566.
●  Also known as “the Lawgiver” (Kanûni) and “the Magnificent” (Muhteşem).
2. Early Life & Rise to Power
●  Süleyman was the only son of Sultan Selim I.
●  Before becoming sultan, he held gove
●  He succeeded his father and became sultan in September 1520.
3. Military Campaigns & Expansion
●  Early in his reign:
○  He captured Belgrade in 1521.
○  He captured Rhodes (1522-23) from the Knights of St. John.
○  At the Battle of Mohács (August 1526) he defeated the Hungarian king Louis II,
significantly weakening Hungary.
●  Siege of Vienna in 1529: He tried to take Vienna from the Habsburgs but had to retreat
due to logistical issues.
●  Eastern campaigns: He fought the Safavid Empire in Persia:
○  First campaign (1534-35): Conquest of Erzurum and Iraq.
○  Second (1548-49): Territory around Lake Van.
○  Third (1554-55): Showed difficulty in subduing Persia.
●  Naval strength: Under his rule the Ottoman navy became powerful, e.g., with Admiral
Khayr al‑Din Barbarossa winning a sea battle off Preveza (1538) against Venice + Spain.
4. Domestic Achievements: Culture, Architecture, Administration
●  Süleyman’s period is considered a “golden age” of the Ottoman Empire for culture,
architecture, law and governance.
●  He surrounded himself with very talented people:
○  Grand Viziers: e.g., İbrahim, Rüstem, Mehmed Sokollu.
○  Religious/legal scholars: e.g., Abū al-Su‘ūd (Hoca Çelebi), Kemalpaşazâde.
○  Poet Bâkî; Architect Mimar Sinan.
●  Public works & city building: He built forts, mosques, bridges, aqueducts, especially in
key cities like Mecca, Damascus, Baghdad.
●  Transformed the city of Istanbul (formerly Constantinople) into a major imperial capital.
5. Legal Reforms & the “Lawgiver” Title
●  One of his most important roles: codifying and simplifying the secular law code (kanûn)
of the empire, while working alongside Islamic law (Sharia).
●  Because of the legal reforms, he earned the nickname “Kanûni” (Lawgiver).
●  These reforms strengthened the central government and helped the empire manage its
large territory more effectively.
6. Diplomacy & Foreign Relations
●  He negotiated with and fought against European powers (e.g., the Habsburgs in
Hungary/Austria).
●  His empire’s reach and influence extended into the Mediterranean, North Africa, the
Middle East, even naval action as far as India’s coast.
7. Later Years & Legacy
●
In his later years there were problems with succession—his sons Mustafa, Selim, and
Bayezid faced conflict, and Mustafa was executed in 1553.
●  His reign ended when he died naturally while on campaign (besieging Szigetvár in
Hungary) in 1566.
●  His legacy: A powerful and expanded empire; a blossoming of Ottoman civilization in art,
architecture, literature, law; often seen as the high point of the Ottoman Empire.
8. Key Dates to Remember
●  1520: Becomes Sultan of the Ottoman Empire.
●  1521: Capture of Belgrade.
●  1522-23: Capture of Rhodes.
●  1526: Battle of Mohács (Hungary).
●  1529: Siege of Vienna.
●  1534-35: Campaign in Persia (Erzurum & Iraq).
●  1548-49: Second Persian campaign.
●  1554-55: Third Persian campaign; naval successes.
●  1566: Süleyman dies.
9. Significance of Suleyman
●  Süleyman’s rule is a classic example of leadership, expansion of empire, law and
governance, and cultural achievement — all themes that appear in IB history.
●  He shows that empires are about much more than just battles: they involve organization,
law, culture, art, and infrastructure.
●  As a long-reigning ruler (46 years) he offers a case study in how one person’s policies
can shape a large empire’s direction.
