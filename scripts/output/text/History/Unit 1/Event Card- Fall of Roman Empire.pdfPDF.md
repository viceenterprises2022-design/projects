Key Events Leading to the Fall of the Western Roman Empire –
Event Cards
🗡️ Assassination of Emperors
Date: 3rd Century CE (Multiple Occurrences)
Description: Roman emperors were frequently overthrown and assassinated by rivals,
soldiers, or political enemies. This created instability and weakened central authority.
Why it mattered: Frequent power struggles made the empire vulnerable to internal
chaos and external threats.
Further Reading: BBC History – Roman Emperors
🏛️ Division of Empire
Date: 285 CE
Description: Emperor Diocletian divided the empire into Eastern and Western halves
to make it easier to govern. While the East (Byzantine Empire) thrived, the West became
weaker.
Why it mattered: This division meant the Western Empire had fewer resources to
defend itself.
Further Reading: National Geographic – Fall of Rome
✝️ Rise of Christianity
Date: 380 CE (Christianity declared the official religion)
Description: Emperor Theodosius made Christianity the official religion of Rome.
Pagan practices declined, and the Church gained influence in politics and society.
Why it mattered: Loyalty shifted from the emperor to the Church, changing the
empire’s identity.
Further Reading: BBC Bitesize – Christianity and Rome
⚔️ Sack of Rome
Date: 410 CE
Description: The Visigoths, led by Alaric, attacked and looted Rome. This was the first
time in 800 years that the city had fallen to a foreign enemy.
Why it mattered: It shocked the empire and symbolized Rome’s declining power.
Further Reading: History Extra – Fall of Rome
🛡️ Barbarian Invasions
Date: 4th–5th Century CE
Description: Germanic tribes, Huns, and Vandals invaded Roman territories, gradually
taking control of large parts of the empire.
Why it mattered: These invasions directly weakened the Western Roman Empire,
leading to its collapse.
Further Reading: World History – Barbarian Tribes
👑 Deposition of Romulus Augustulus
Date: 476 CE
Description: The last Roman emperor, Romulus Augustulus, was deposed by the
Germanic leader Odoacer. This event is traditionally considered the end of the Western
Roman Empire.
Why it mattered: It marked the official fall of the Western Roman Empire and the start
of the Middle Ages in Europe.
Further Reading: Britannica – Fall of Rome
