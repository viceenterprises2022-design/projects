Jerusalem – A City of Many Faiths and Communities
Why Jerusalem is Special
Jerusalem is one of the world’s oldest cities and has been continuously inhabited for about 5,000 years. It is considered a holy city by the three major Abrahamic religions — Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. Because of its religious importance, people in the city are often grouped by their religion and tend to live in separate neighborhoods.
Ancient & Medieval History
Around 1000 BCE, King David made Jerusalem the capital of the Jewish kingdom of Israel.
His son, King Solomon, built the First Temple, which became the center of Jewish worship.
In 70 CE, the Romans destroyed the Second Temple and much of the city, a major turning point in Jewish history.
Over centuries, Jerusalem was ruled by Romans, Byzantines, Muslims, Crusaders, and Ottomans, each leaving their mark on the city’s architecture and culture.
Jews in Jerusalem
Jews are the largest and most diverse community, with roots from across the world.
Main groups include Ashkenazim (European origin), Sephardim (Spanish/Portuguese origin), and Mizrahim (Middle Eastern/North African origin).
Religious diversity exists too: secular Jews, traditional Jews, and ultra-Orthodox Jews.
Important holy places include:
The Western Wall (remains of the Second Temple).
The Temple Mount (location of the First and Second Temples).
The Mount of Olives, with ancient Jewish cemeteries.
Jerusalem is also a global center of Jewish religious learning.
Muslims in Jerusalem
Almost all are Sunni Muslims of Arab origin.
Jerusalem is Islam’s third holiest city after Mecca and Medina.
Their most sacred site is the Al-Haram al-Sharif (Noble Sanctuary), which includes the Dome of the Rock and the Al-Aqsa Mosque.
Tradition says the Prophet Muhammad ascended to heaven from this site.
In the past, there was a famous local pilgrimage called Nabi Musa.
Today, Muslim institutions in East Jerusalem are partly managed by local councils and the Palestinian Authority.
Christians in Jerusalem
Christians are the smallest group but the most diverse, with many denominations.
Most are Arab Christians, but the city is home to Greek Orthodox, Roman Catholic, Armenian, Coptic, Ethiopian, Protestant, and other groups.
The holiest Christian site is the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, believed to be the place where Jesus was crucified, buried, and resurrected.
The Armenian Quarter of the Old City is home to one of the world’s oldest Armenian communities.
Population Changes Over Time
In Ottoman times (before the 20th century), the population was relatively small, but Jews slowly became the largest group.
By the late 19th century, Jewish immigration made them the majority.
After 1948, many Arabs, especially Christians, left the city following the Arab–Israeli war.
In 1967, during the Six-Day War, Israel captured East Jerusalem and unified the city under its control.
Since then, Jerusalem’s population has more than doubled. Today, Jews make up about two-thirds, while Muslims (mostly Arabs) are the fastest-growing group.
Jerusalem’s families tend to be large, especially among Muslims and ultra-Orthodox Jews.
Modern Recognition & Geography
In 1981, the Old City of Jerusalem was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
Jerusalem sits at an elevation of about 2,500 feet (760 meters) above sea level.The city remains a focal point of religious pilgrimage, cultural identity, and political conflict, drawing millions of visitors and worshippers each year.
