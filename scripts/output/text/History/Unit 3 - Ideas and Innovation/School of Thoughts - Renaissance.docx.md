Renaissance – School of Thought
Humanism
Scenario: A young scholar in Florence spends hours poring over ancient Greek and Roman texts, studying philosophy, literature, and history. Inspired by the wisdom of the ancients, he writes poetry celebrating the beauty of human existence and the importance of individualism.
Definition: Humanism was an intellectual movement of the Renaissance that emphasized the study of classical texts, such as those of ancient Greece and Rome. Humanists sought to revive classical learning and promote human potential, focusing on subjects like literature, history, philosophy, and the arts. They valued reason, education, and the importance of individual achievement.
Classicism
Scenario: A renowned artist in Venice meticulously studies the works of ancient sculptors and painters, such as Phidias and Polykleitos. He incorporates their techniques and ideals of beauty into his own art, creating sculptures and paintings that reflect the harmony, balance, and proportion of classical antiquity.
Definition: Classicism refers to the artistic and intellectual movement of the Renaissance that drew inspiration from the art, literature, and philosophy of ancient Greece and Rome. Classicists sought to emulate the ideals of beauty, harmony, and proportion found in classical art and literature, believing that these principles represented the highest standards of artistic excellence.
Heliocentrism
Scenario: A mathematician and astronomer in Poland spends years observing the movements of the planets and stars. Through meticulous calculations and observations, he develops a revolutionary theory that places the sun, rather than the Earth, at the center of the solar system.
Definition: Heliocentrism is the astronomical model that places the sun at the center of the solar system, with the planets, including Earth, orbiting around it. This concept challenged the geocentric model, which placed Earth at the center of the universe, and was a significant development in Renaissance science, leading to a better understanding of the cosmos.
Rationalism
Scenario: A philosopher in Florence engages in deep contemplation and reasoned inquiry, seeking to understand the nature of reality and the universe. He believes that knowledge can be attained through logical reasoning and deductive thought, independent of sensory experience.
Definition: Rationalism is a philosophical school of thought that emphasizes the role of reason and intellect in acquiring knowledge and understanding the world. Rationalists believe that certain truths can be known a priori, or independently of sensory experience, through logical deduction and intuition. This approach to knowledge contrasts with empiricism, which emphasizes the importance of sensory experience and observation.
Empiricism
Scenario: A scientist in Padua conducts experiments to investigate the properties of matter and motion. He meticulously observes, measures, and records the results of his experiments, believing that knowledge is derived from sensory experience and empirical evidence.
Definition: Empiricism is a philosophical and scientific approach that emphasizes the importance of sensory experience, observation, and experimentation in acquiring knowledge. Empiricists believe that all knowledge comes from experience, and that the mind is a blank slate (tabula rasa) at birth, which is gradually filled with knowledge through sensory perception and interaction with the world.
