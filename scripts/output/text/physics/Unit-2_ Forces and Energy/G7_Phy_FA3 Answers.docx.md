Grade 7 FA 3
Criterion A (Knowledge and Understanding)
1. Define the following forces in your own words:
Force:
A push or a pull on an object, resulting from its interaction with another object, which causes it to change its velocity.
Gravity:
A non-contact force that pulls all objects toward the center of the Earth or any other mass.
Friction:
A contact force that resists motion between two surfaces that are touching each other.
Normal force:
A contact force exerted by a surface that supports the weight of an object resting on it and acts perpendicular to that surface.
2. Classify the following as contact or non-contact forces:
Magnetic – Non-contact
Push – Contact
Tension – Contact
Electrostatic – Non-contact
Air resistance – Contact
Criterion B (Inquiring and Designing)
3. Experiment on air resistance
• Hypothesis:
If two objects of the same mass but different shapes are dropped from the same height, the object with the larger surface area will fall more slowly because it experiences greater air resistance.
• Materials required:
Two sheets of paper (one crumpled, one flat)
Stopwatch
Measuring tape or ruler
Chair or table to drop from
• Two steps of experimental plan:
Measure and hold both paper sheets at the same height. Drop them at the same time and observe which one reaches the ground first.
Repeat the experiment three times for accuracy and record the time taken for each object to fall.
Criterion D (Reflecting on the Impact of Science)
1. Summarise the science:
When you sit still on a chair, the downward gravitational force on your body is exactly balanced by the upward normal force from the chair. Because these forces are equal and opposite, there is no movement — the system is in equilibrium.
2. Describe the implications:
If the forces become unbalanced, the chair could tip over or collapse, causing you to fall and possibly get injured. This shows why balanced forces are important for stability and safety in furniture design.
3. Evaluate the design:
A well-designed chair evenly distributes weight and keeps forces balanced, preventing it from tipping. If the chair were poorly designed or unstable, it could easily topple when someone sits on it, increasing the risk of accidents.
