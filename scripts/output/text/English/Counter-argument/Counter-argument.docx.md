Q: What is an argument?
An argument is a statement that expresses an opinion and gives reasons or evidence to support it. (Argument = opinion + reason)
Q: What is a counter-argument?
A counter-argument is a statement that challenges or opposes the main argument. (Counter-argument = opinion + refutation of the argument’s reason)
Q: Why is it important to learn to write counter-arguments?
We use counter-arguments in debates and dissertations for research purposes. In debates, counter-arguments help us question and persuade others. In dissertations, they help clarify our concepts and make our writing clearer and more objective.
Q: How do we frame a counter-argument?
Step 1: Acknowledge the argument by explaining the connection between the opinion and the reason.
Step 2: Question or challenge the reason with new evidence or research, or knowledge from your own experience. This becomes your refutation.
Step 3: Conclude the counter-argument by restating the refutation and use it to frame your opinion based on your reason that is opposed to the main argument.

Argument
Students should be allowed to use mobile phones in school because they can support learning in many useful ways. Phones allow quick access to dictionaries, educational websites, and videos that help students understand lessons better. They can also be used to take pictures of notes or record homework reminders. Learning to use phones responsibly prepares students for a future where technology is everywhere. Instead of banning them, schools should teach students how to use their devices wisely to make learning more engaging and independent.

Counter-argument
Some people argue that students should be allowed to use mobile phones because they provide easy access to learning tools and platforms. This ease of access itself does more harm than good. This is because phones can easily distract students from lessons, tempt them to text friends, or even lead to cheating during tests. Teachers already have better tools for learning, like laptops or tablets that are monitored. Allowing phones could make classroom management harder and reduce focus. While some believe phones can help with research, their potential for distraction outweighs the benefits, especially for younger students who are still learning self-control.
