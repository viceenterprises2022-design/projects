Poem: The Migrant by A L Hendriks
What is Tone in a Poem?
Tone is the poet’s attitude toward the subject they are writing about.It tells us how the speaker feels—angry, hopeful, sad, mocking, grateful, fearful, exhausted, etc.
Look for:
Adjectives (harsh, weary, tired, hopeful, bright, bleak)
Verbs (drift, march, flee, cling)
Figurative language (metaphors, personification, irony)
Descriptive details (weather, environment, movement, dialogue)
How to identify tone
Ask:
What feelings or attitude does the speaker show?
Are they judging the situation or simply describing it?
Do they sound emotional or detached?
Tone often shifts, so noticing changes is important.
What is Mood in a Poem?
Mood is the feeling the poem creates in the reader.It is about the emotional atmosphere — calm, tense, gloomy, nostalgic, peaceful, uncertain, joyful.
Look for:
Imagery (visual pictures, sounds, smells)
Setting (place, time, weather)
Repetition (creates anxiety, rhythm, or calm)
Diction (word choice that feels heavy, light, dark, bright)
How to identify mood
Ask:
What emotions do I feel while reading?
What images or words create these emotions?
Mood is created in the reader, but guided by the language the poet chooses.
Key difference
Tone = Poet/Speaker’s attitude
Mood = Reader’s emotional response
Tone comes from the writer.Mood happens in the reader.
