What was the American Civil War?
The American Civil War was fought in the United States between 1861 and 1865.
It was a war between:
The Union (Northern states, led by the U.S. federal government)
The Confederacy (Southern states that seceded and formed the Confederate States of America)
Why Did the War Begin?
Main Causes
Slavery – The most important issue.
Southern states depended on enslaved labor for agriculture (especially cotton). Many Northern states opposed the expansion of slavery into new territories.
States’ Rights – Southern states argued that individual states had the right to decide their own laws, including whether to allow slavery.
Economic Differences –
North: Industrial, manufacturing-based economy
South: Agricultural, plantation-based economy
Election of 1860 –
The election of Abraham Lincoln, who opposed the spread of slavery, led Southern states to fear their political power was weakening.
Secession and the Start of War
December 1860: South Carolina became the first state to secede.
Eventually, 11 Southern states formed the Confederacy.
The Confederacy elected Jefferson Davis as its president.
April 12, 1861
War officially began when Confederate forces fired on Fort Sumter in South Carolina.
Who Fought?
Approximately:
2.1 million soldiers fought for the Union
About 1 million soldiers fought for the Confederacy
Many soldiers were teenagers — some as young as 13 or 14 served as drummer boys, messengers, or helpers. This directly connects to Joby in Bradbury’s story.
The Battle of Shiloh (April 6–7, 1862)
Battle of Shiloh
Took place in Tennessee, near the Tennessee River.
Named after a small church called “Shiloh,” meaning place of peace in Hebrew — an ironic name considering the violence.
Why It Was Important:
One of the first major battles in the Western Theater of the war.
Shocked the nation because of the enormous casualties.
Casualties at Shiloh:
Around 23,000 soldiers killed, wounded, or missing in just two days.
At that time, it was the bloodiest battle in American history.
Bradbury sets Drummer Boy of Shiloh on the night before this battle to capture the fear and uncertainty before such massive loss.
Key Leaders
Abraham Lincoln – President of the Union
Jefferson Davis – President of the Confederacy
Ulysses S. Grant – Major Union general (fought at Shiloh)
Robert E. Lee – Leading Confederate general
How Did the War End?
April 9, 1865
Confederate General Robert E. Lee surrendered to Union General Ulysses S. Grant at Appomattox Court House in Virginia.
April 14, 1865
President Abraham Lincoln was assassinated just five days after the surrender.
Casualties and Impact
Approximately 620,000–750,000 soldiers died (modern estimates suggest even higher).
More Americans died in the Civil War than in World War I and World War II combined.
Slavery was abolished through the 13th Amendment (1865).
Why Is the American Civil War So Memorable?
🔹 It Redefined the United States
Ended slavery legally.
Strengthened federal government power over states.
🔹 It Was Brother Against Brother
Families were divided.
Some states had citizens fighting on both sides.
🔹 The Scale of Death Was Shocking
Before this war, Americans had never experienced loss on this level.
Interesting Facts about the setting of The Drummer Boy
Drummer boys were often under 16 years old. They did not usually carry weapons but were essential for communication.
Battles were sometimes fought near farms, orchards, and churches — just like in Bradbury’s story.
Many soldiers thought the war would last only a few weeks. It lasted four years.
The name “Shiloh” means peace, yet it became one of the bloodiest battlefields.
Medical knowledge was limited — more soldiers died from disease than from battle wounds.
