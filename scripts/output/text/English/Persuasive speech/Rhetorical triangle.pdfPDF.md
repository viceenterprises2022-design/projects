What is Rhetoric?
Aristotle first defined rhetoric as “the faculty of observing in any given
case the available means of persuasion.”
Basically this means, the skill of choosing the best way
to persuade people — by using reason, credibility, or
emotion — depending on the situation.
The Rhetorical Triangle
Ethos
Ethos refers to the credibility of the
speaker. This can be achieved by the
speaker showcasing their expertise
or authority on the topic. A speaker's
personal character can also help
create trust with an audience.
Logos refers to the
used of logical
reasoning, evidence,
and rational
arguments. Logos is
fact-driven and is
most effective with a
logical audience.
Logos
Pathos
Pathos is when a speaker
uses emotional appeals
on the audience. The
emotions that a speaker
can stir in an audiences
ranges from sadness and
sympathy to empowered
and inspired.

Remember, the
rhetorical triangle is
an analytical tool
and not a technique.
With it, we analyse
how persuasion
works in a speech
or text overall.
A technique, on the
other hand, is
specific and
sentence-based.
