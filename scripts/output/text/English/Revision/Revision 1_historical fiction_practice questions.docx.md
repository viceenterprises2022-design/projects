Criterion A: Analysing
Q1. Identify two words or phrases from the poem that show water is very important to the people. You only need to name the words or phrases and cite the lines from the poem where these words occur. No long explanation is needed.
(Tip: Look for words that show water is precious or special. Copy the lines exactly from the poem)
Q2. Analyse how one image in the poem shows the problem of water shortage. Explain how the image works and why the poet used it.
(Tip: Talk about what the image shows, cite the specific line which you have chosen. Explain what it makes us think or feel)
Criterion B: Organising
Q3. Discuss how the poem moves from dryness to joy. Write about both parts of the poem — the beginning and the later part. Cite the specific lines you have chosen to discuss.
(Tip: Start with the dry beginning – copy the lines and explain how they indicate dryness. Then explain what changes when water arrives. Again, copy the specific lines and explain their impact)
Q4. Comment on how the poet groups people together in the poem. Share what you notice and what you think about it. Comment means you use your own words to decode the deeper meaning.
(Tip: Look for words that show many people together. Cite the specific lines containing these words. Use your own words to explain why the poet shows them as one group.)
Criterion C: Producing Text
Q5. Create four lines of poetry showing water as something precious. Write your own short poem inspired by the ideas in Blessing.
(Tip: Think of water like gold, music, or light. Use simple words and one image that you relate to)
Q6. Create a short paragraph describing how children react to water in the poem. Write in your own words what the children do and how they feel.
(Tip: Use action words (run, shout, play). Imagine the scene like a picture.)
Criterion D: Using Language
Q7. Explore how religious words are used to describe water in the poem. Look closely at the words and explain what they suggest.
(Tip: Find words linked to religion. Explain how they make water feel special)
Q8. Analyse the effect of one poetic device on the reader. Explain how a poetic technique makes the reader feel or think.
(Tip: Name the device first (simile, metaphor, imagery). Then say how it affects the reader)
