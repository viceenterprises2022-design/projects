Blessing (By Imtiaz Dharker)  - annotated version
The skin cracks like a pod.
(Poetic Device – Simile, compares dry skin to a cracked seed pod. Tone: Shows extreme dryness and suffering.
Mood - Creates a harsh, uncomfortable mood.)
There never is enough water.
(Direct, simple statement – also called declarative statement. Emphasises scarcity. Tone - Deprivation and realism.)
Imagine the drip of it,
(Invites the reader to imagine water. Shows how rare water is—it exists mainly in imagination. Mood: longing, desire.)
the small splash, echo
(Poetic device: Auditory imagery (drip, splash, echo). Shows how even a small amount of water matters. Mood: quiet, fragile hope)
in a tin mug,
(Auditory and visual Imagery: drip, splash, echo. Shows how even a small amount of water matters. Mood: quiet, fragile hope.)
the voice of a kindly god.
(Metaphor: water compared to a god. Suggests water brings life, mercy, and blessing. Tone becomes reverent.)
Sometimes, the sudden rush
(The tone shifts from reality to anticipation: “Sudden” suggests surprise and change. Builds anticipation.)
of fortune. The municipal pipe bursts,
(Irony: Broken infrastructure becomes a blessing. It highlights the distance between the haves and the have-nots. The privileged classes would complain about the pipe burst while the slum dwellers welcome it. Shows poverty and neglect. Tone mixes joy of the have-nots with social critique of the haves)
silver crashes to the ground
(Metaphor: water as silver (precious). Also, visual imagery. Tone: Verb crashes shows power and excitement. Mood becomes energetic and joyful.)
and the flow has found
(Personification: water “roars”. Suggests abundance and life)
a roar of tongues. From the huts,
(Communal joy is emphasised. Tone: Focus shifts from water and infrastructure to the people who are experiencing that infrastructure. Simple phrase grounds the poem in reality. Symbolism: Huts as a symbol of poverty)
a congregation: every man woman
(Metaphor: Religious word congregation. Shows unity and shared joy. Water brings people together – just as religion does.)
child for streets around
(Shows the scale of the moment. The blessing spreads beyond one place.
butts in, with pots,
(Everyday objects emphasise realism. Urgency and eagerness shown.
brass, copper, aluminium,
(Listing of materials. Highlights scarcity—people collect whatever they can. Shows socioeconomic conditions)
plastic buckets,
(Modern, cheap materials. Reinforces poverty and adaptability.)
frantic hands,
(imagery: The adjective “frantic” immediately conveys urgency and desperation. These hands are not calm or orderly; they are rushed and anxious. Synecdoche (part for the whole and whole for the part) — “hands” stand for whole people, reducing individuals to their need, highlighting poverty and urgency. Tone: This line sharpens the tone into one of panic mixed with need. The people are scrambling to collect as much water as possible because they know it is rare and temporary. Mood: We feel the intensity of the moment — water is precious, and no one can afford to miss this chance.
and naked children
(Imagery: The image of “naked children” is stark and confronting. Symbolism: Nakedness suggests poverty, vulnerability, and innocence, not shame. It also suggests freedom — children unconcerned with social rules, responding instinctively to joy. Tone shifts from adult desperation to childlike immediacy and openness. Dharker presents this image plainly, without judgement, forcing the reader to confront inequality. Mood: a mixture of tenderness, discomfort, and empathy.
screaming in the liquid sun,
(Auditory imagery: “screaming” conveys unfiltered joy — loud, uncontrolled, spontaneous. Metaphor: “liquid sun” blends water and light, suggesting that the water is as life-giving and radiant as sunlight. Mood: The mood here is ecstatic and celebratory, contrasting sharply with the earlier dryness. Dharker combines sound (screaming), sight (sun), and touch (liquid), creating multi-sensory imagery. The children experience water not as scarcity but as pure delight.
their highlights polished to perfection,
(Visual imagery: “highlights” refers to the way light reflects off wet skin. Metaphor: “polished to perfection” compares the children’s bodies to valuable objects being carefully cleaned or refined. Irony: The children are materially poor, yet visually they appear beautiful and radiant. This contrasts wealth and poverty — beauty and joy exist without material riches, rather, the idea that material wealth is relative. For the haves, access to water is taken for granted. For the have-nots, access to water is a moment of celebration. Tone: The tone becomes almost reverent, as if the poet is pausing to admire the scene. Effect: The contrast encourages the reader to see dignity and beauty in overlooked lives.
Flashing light,
(The short, isolated phrase mirrors the quick, flickering movement of light. Imagery: Suggests brightness, motion, and joy. Mood: The mood is lively and exhilarating. Technique: Ellipsis / omission — the lack of a full sentence speeds up the poem and mimics the chaos and excitement of the moment. Symbolism: Light here represents hope, life, and momentary transcendence.
as the blessing sings
(Personification: The “blessing” (water) is given a human voice. Metaphor: Water is no longer just liquid — it becomes something alive, musical, and sacred. Religious connotation: The word “blessing” reinforces spiritual overtones; water is almost divine. Tone: Deeply reverent and celebratory. This line suggests that the moment is more than physical relief — it is emotionally and spiritually uplifting.
over their small bones.
Visual Imagery: “small bones” reminds us of the children’s fragility. Contrast: Joy (singing blessing) Vulnerability (small bones). Meaning towards social realism: Bones suggest thinness, malnutrition, long-term hardship. Tone shift: The poem quietly reintroduces seriousness beneath the joy. The line prevents the poem from becoming sentimental. The reader is left aware that joy does not erase poverty. Mood: A blend of wonder and sadness, encouraging reflection rather than simple happiness.
