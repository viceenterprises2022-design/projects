Below are four questions from the dystopian novel, Fahrenheit 451 by Ray Bradbury. To help you practice, we are breaking down the command terms in this revision sheet.
Q:1 — Analyse: Analyse how Montag’s relationship with Clarisse contributes to his transformation in the novel.
How to answer (Command Term: Analyse):
To analyse means to break the idea into parts, look at the reasons behind them, and explain how they connect.
For this question:
Identify key moments between Montag and Clarisse
Explain why each moment matters (give at least two)
Show how these moments change Montag’s thoughts or actions
Link the change to the bigger themes of the book
Your answer:
CRITERION B — ORGANISING
Q:2 — Identify: Identify key similarities and differences between Mildred and Clarisse that show their contrasting roles in the story.
How to answer (Command Term: Identify):
To identify means to pick out, name, or list important features without long explanations. For this question:
List at least 2 similarities
List at least 2 differences
Keep your points short and clear (e.g., “Clarisse is curious; Mildred is apathetic.”)
CRITERION C — PRODUCING TEXT
Q:3 Discuss - Discuss the significance of the old woman’s choice to burn with her books.
How to answer (Command Term: Discuss): To discuss means to examine different sides or ideas and give a balanced explanation.
For this question:
Explain why her act matters in the story
Explain how it affects Montag
Explain what it shows about censorship or belief
Explore emotional, moral, or symbolic meanings
Q:4 — Comment: Comment on how the use of strong, precise vocabulary can make a description of Montag’s emotions more powerful.
How to answer (Command Term: Comment): To comment means to give your opinion with reasons, not just describe.
Here you should:
Explain why specific or precise words are better than vague ones
Give examples of strong words versus weak words
Show how better vocabulary changes the impact of the text on the reader
