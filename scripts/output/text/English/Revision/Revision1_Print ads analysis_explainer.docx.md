Simplified Explainer: Analysing Print Advertisements
1. The Rhetorical Triangle: The rhetorical triangle helps us understand how advertisements persuade us. It has three parts:
a. Ethos – Trust: Advertisers try to show that they are trustworthy, expert, or reliable. They want you to think: “I can believe this brand!”
b. Pathos – Feelings: They use techniques that make you feel something – excitement, fear, happiness, hope, or even sadness. Feelings push us to buy.
c. Logos – Facts & Logic: Advertisers use facts, numbers, or reasons to show that their product is helpful or better.
2. Four Common Advertising Techniques: These are tricks or methods that print ads use to catch attention and convince people.
a. Fear Appeal: The ad makes you feel scared or worried about something so that you buy the product to stay safe.
Example: "If you don’t use this toothpaste, your teeth may decay!"
b. Tall Claims: The ad makes big, exaggerated promises that are usually impossible to prove.
Example: "This cream will make you look younger in 24 hours!"
c. Bandwagon: The ad suggests that everyone is using this product, so you should too.
Example: "Join millions who have switched to EcoWater bottles!"
d. Testimonial: The ad uses a famous person or a real user who says the product is good.
Example: A cricketer promoting an energy drink.
Quick Tips for Analysis
When you see a print advertisement, ask:
What feeling is this ad trying to create? (Pathos)
Why should I trust this brand? (Ethos)
What facts or reasons are they giving me? (Logos)
Which technique is used: fear appeal, tall claim, bandwagon, or testimonial?
