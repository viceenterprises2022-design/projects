Blessing (By Imtiaz Dharker)
The skin cracks like a pod.
There never is enough water.
Imagine the drip of it,
the small splash, echo
in a tin mug,
the voice of a kindly god.
Sometimes, the sudden rush
of fortune. The municipal pipe bursts,
silver crashes to the ground
and the flow has found
a roar of tongues. From the huts,
a congregation: every man woman
child for streets around
butts in, with pots,
brass, copper, aluminium,
plastic buckets,
frantic hands,
and naked children
screaming in the liquid sun,
their highlights polished to perfection,
flashing light,
as the blessing sings
over their small bones.
Who Is Imtiaz Dharker? — Biography & Literary Profile
Imtiaz Dharker is a Pakistani-born British poet, artist, and documentary filmmaker. She was born on 31 January 1954 in Lahore, Pakistan, and grew up in Glasgow, Scotland after her family moved there when she was an infant.
Over her long and distinguished career, Dharker has become one of the most respected contemporary poets writing in English. Her work has received major honours — including the Queen’s Gold Medal for Poetry (2014), election as a Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature (2011), and she has served as Chancellor of Newcastle University since 2020.
The poem “Blessing” appears in the collection Postcards from God, which was published in the 1990s (mid-1990s) and later republished with other early work. Different sources place the poem’s origin within Dharker’s early published work in the late 1980s or 1990s (e.g., it was associated with her earlier Purdah (1989) and later included in Postcards from God).
Dharker’s poems, including “Blessing,” are widely anthologised and studied, and the poem is included in British GCSE and IGCSE anthologies used in English education in the UK and internationally.
Across her work, Dharker explores several recurring themes:
Home and displacement
Freedom and journeys
Geographical and cultural identity
Social inequality and communal life
Gender politics and human rights
These themes arise out of her personal life experiences — growing up between cultures (Pakistan and Scotland), living in Mumbai (Bombay) for many years, and observing urban life and poverty firsthand. Her poetry often reflects the cross-cultural tensions of global modern life as well as very real historical and social conditions that people experience — such as poverty, scarcity, migration, and survival.
The poem’s relevance to the unit – Historical fiction
“Blessing” is set in an urban slum in Mumbai, where basic infrastructure (such as water supply) is unreliable. This reflects real historical and social conditions of urban life in many developing cities. The poem’s depiction of scarcity and the way communities respond to it gives students insight into:
how people lived,
how environmental conditions shaped daily life, and
how historical realities affect human experience.
This is similar to historical fiction, where imagined characters live through real conditions. Dharker’s poem imagines voices reacting to scarcity and survival. The experience of water shortage, the sense of migration and temporary accommodation in areas like Dharavi (Asia’s biggest slum), and the urgency of communal response all connect to larger histories of urbanisation, migration, and inequality. As a student, you can make meaningful text–world connections between the poem and broader historical forces — just as in historical fiction.
The poem uses personal perspective to illuminate shared experience
Like many classic historical fiction texts, Dharker’s work gives a personal, human voice to conditions that are real and historically situated. Instead of telling a dry fact, the poem’s speaker allows readers to feel the lived experience of these conditions, much as historical fiction does.
The poem’s imagery — such as an old municipal pipe bursting, children playing in the sudden water, and adults scrambling to collect it — points to everyday realities shaped by economic and social history. Though Dharker does not name a specific year in the poem, the urban landscape she evokes — inconsistent water access, slum-like settlement patterns — is rooted in documented social realities, especially around the city of Mumbai where she has lived and worked.
In this way, “Blessing” not only describes a moment but also situates it within a historically informed lived experience — much like the characters in a historical fiction narrative whose lives are shaped by real social forces.
