To design a logical, complete, and safe method to detect the presence of carbon dioxide as
the by-product of respiration, you will need the following materials and equipment:
Aim: The aim of this experiment is to demonstrate the presence of carbon dioxide in the air
we breathe out using a logical, complete, and safe method.
Materials:
•  Lime water (calcium hydroxide solution)
•  glass test tube or bottle with a lid
•  Clear drinking straw
•  Small funnel
Equipment:
•  Graduated cylinder or pipette for measuring liquids
•  Lab Coats, safety goggles and gloves
Method:
•  Put on lab coat, safety goggles and gloves.
•  Measure 30 ml of lime water using a graduated cylinder or pipette and pour it into
the beaker.
•  Breathe into the clear drinking straw, and then blow into the lime water in the
conical flask for about 20 seconds.
•  Quickly cover the test tube or bottle with the lid.
•  Shake the test tube or bottle gently to ensure thorough mixing of the air and lime
water.
•  Observe the test tube or bottle for the formation of a cloudy precipitate of calcium
carbonate. If carbon dioxide is present, the lime water will turn cloudy, indicating the
presence of carbon dioxide.
Reaction: Ca(OH)2 (Calcium Hydroxide)  + CO2 (Carbon dioxide) === CaCO3 (Calcium
Carbonate) + H2O(Water)
Safety Precautions:
•  Perform the experiment in a well-ventilated area or under a fume hood to avoid
inhaling any potentially harmful gases.
•  Handle the limewater (calcium hydroxide solution) with care and avoid contact with
skin or eyes, as it can cause irritation.
•  Avoid directly exhaling into the limewater, as it may contain other potentially
harmful substances from your breath. Use a straw or glass tube to direct your breath
into the limewater.
•  Always follow appropriate safety guidelines and regulations when conducting
experiments, and wear appropriate protective gear, such as gloves and goggles, as
needed.
