1. Breathing Rate
Meaning of Breathing Rate
Breathing rate means the number of breaths a person takes in one minute.
One breath includes:
breathing in + breathing out
Example
If a person breathes in and out 18 times in one minute, their breathing rate is:
18 breaths per minute
Normal Breathing Rate
A normal resting breathing rate for many people is around:
12–20 breaths per minute
This can change depending on age, activity, health, and fitness.
Why Breathing Rate Increases During Exercise
During exercise, body cells need more energy.
To release more energy, cells need more oxygen for respiration.
They also produce more carbon dioxide, which must be removed from the body.
So, breathing becomes:
•
faster
•  deeper
•  more frequent
Simple explanation
When you run, your muscles work harder. They need more oxygen to release energy.
Therefore, you breathe faster to bring in more oxygen and remove extra carbon dioxide.

2. Respiration
Meaning of Respiration
Respiration is the chemical process by which cells release energy from food.
The food mainly used is glucose.
Word Equation for Aerobic Respiration
Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy
Balanced Chemical Equation
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy
Why Respiration Is Important
Respiration releases energy for:
•  movement
•  growth
•
repair of cells
•  keeping warm
•  heartbeat
•  breathing
•  brain activity
3. Respirometer
What Is a Respirometer?
A respirometer is an apparatus used to measure the rate of respiration in living organisms.
It helps us study how much oxygen an organism uses during respiration.
Purpose of a Respirometer
A respirometer is used to:
1.  measure the rate of respiration
2.  measure oxygen uptake by an organism
3.  compare respiration rates under different conditions
4.  investigate how factors such as temperature or activity affect respiration
Basic Setup of a Respirometer
A simple respirometer usually has:
Function
Part

Soda lime or potassium hydroxide  Absorbs carbon dioxide

Rubber bung

Why Soda Lime Is Used
During respiration, the organism:
•  uses oxygen
•  produces carbon dioxide
The soda lime absorbs the carbon dioxide produced.
Because carbon dioxide is absorbed, the total gas volume inside the tube decreases as oxygen
is used.
This decrease causes the coloured liquid bubble to move.
How a Respirometer Works
1.  The organism respires.
2.  It uses oxygen from the air inside the chamber.
3.  It produces carbon dioxide.
4.  Soda lime absorbs the carbon dioxide.
5.  Since oxygen is used up and carbon dioxide is absorbed, the gas volume decreases.
6.  This causes the coloured liquid bubble to move along the capillary tube.
7.  The movement of the bubble shows the amount of oxygen used.
Basic Respirometer Method
Step-by-step method
1.  Place a small living organism, such as a germinating seed or small insect, in the
respirometer chamber.
2.  Add soda lime or potassium hydroxide to absorb carbon dioxide.
3.  Use cotton wool to prevent the organism from touching the soda lime.
4.  Seal the apparatus tightly with a rubber bung.
5.  Place a coloured liquid bubble in the capillary tube.
6.  Record the starting position of the bubble.
7.  Wait for a fixed time, such as 5 minutes.
8.  Record the final position of the bubble.
9.  Calculate the distance moved by the bubble.
10. Use the distance or volume change to estimate respiration rate.
Safety Note
If using small animals, they must be handled carefully and humanely. They should not be
harmed and should not be kept in the apparatus for too long.

5. Lime Water Test
What Is Lime Water?
Lime water is a clear solution of calcium hydroxide.
It is used to test for carbon dioxide gas.
Purpose of the Lime Water Test
The lime water test is used to show whether carbon dioxide is present.
In respiration, carbon dioxide is produced as a waste product.
So, the lime water test can show that respiration is taking place.
How to Perform the Lime Water Test
Materials needed
lime water
test tube
•
•
•  straw or delivery tube
•  exhaled air or gas sample
Method
1.  Pour a small amount of clear lime water into a test tube.
2.  Use a straw or delivery tube to bubble exhaled air through the lime water.
3.  Observe any colour change.
4.  If carbon dioxide is present, the lime water turns milky or cloudy.
Observation
Clear lime water turns milky/cloudy.
Result
Carbon dioxide is present.
Conclusion
Respiration produces carbon dioxide.
When we breathe out, we release carbon dioxide produced by respiration in our cells.
Lime Water Test Equation
Carbon dioxide reacts with calcium hydroxide in lime water to form calcium carbonate.
Calcium hydroxide + Carbon dioxide → Calcium carbonate + Water
Calcium carbonate is insoluble, so it makes the lime water appear milky.

6. Lime Water Test and Respiration
During respiration:
Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy
Carbon dioxide is produced as a waste gas.
When this carbon dioxide is passed through lime water, the lime water turns milky.
This proves that respiration produces carbon dioxide.

8. Three Negative Health Effects of Smoking
1. Smoking damages the lungs
Smoking damages the airways and air sacs in the lungs.
This can cause:
•  coughing
•  breathlessness
•
lung infections
•  bronchitis
•  emphysema
•
lung cancer
2. Smoking reduces oxygen supply
Cigarette smoke contains carbon monoxide.
Carbon monoxide reduces the ability of blood to carry oxygen.
As a result:
•
less oxygen reaches body cells
•
the person feels tired easily
•  exercise becomes difficult
•  breathing becomes harder
3. Smoking increases the risk of heart disease
Nicotine makes the heart beat faster.
It can also increase blood pressure.
This makes the heart work harder.
Long-term smoking can increase the risk of:
•  heart disease
•  stroke
•  damaged blood vessels
4. Smoking can cause cancer
Tar and other chemicals in cigarette smoke damage body cells.
Damaged cells may begin to divide uncontrollably.
This can lead to cancer, especially lung cancer.
5. Smoking weakens the immune system
Smoking makes the body less able to fight infections.
Smokers may get:
•  coughs
•  colds
•  chest infections
•  slower healing
9. How Smoking Impacts the Respiratory System
The respiratory system includes:
•  nose
•
trachea
•  bronchi
•
lungs
•  alveoli
Smoking mainly damages the airways and lungs.
A. Smoking damages cilia
The air passages have tiny hair-like structures called cilia.
Normal function of cilia
Cilia move mucus, dust, and germs out of the airways.
Effect of smoking
Smoking damages or destroys cilia.
Result
Mucus, dust, and germs remain trapped in the airways.
This causes:
•  coughing
•  mucus build-up
•  chest infections
B. Smoking increases mucus production
Smoke irritates the lining of the airways.
The body produces extra mucus.
Result
The airways become blocked with mucus.
This makes breathing harder and causes a smoker’s cough.
C. Smoking inflames the bronchi
The bronchi are tubes that carry air into the lungs.
Smoking irritates and inflames the bronchi.
Result
The bronchi become narrower.
Less air can pass through them.
This can lead to bronchitis.
D. Smoking damages alveoli
The alveoli are tiny air sacs in the lungs.
Normal function of alveoli
They allow oxygen to enter the blood and carbon dioxide to leave the blood.
Effect of smoking
Smoking damages the walls of the alveoli.
Result
There is less surface area for gas exchange.
Less oxygen enters the blood.
The person becomes breathless more quickly.
This can lead to emphysema.
E. Smoking increases risk of lung cancer
Tar and other chemicals in smoke damage lung cells.
If cells are damaged badly, they may become cancerous.
