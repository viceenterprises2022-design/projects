Global Distribution and Climate Patterns
Tropical grasslands, also known as savannas, are found mainly on either side of the
Equator, between tropical rainforests and deserts. Major savanna regions include East
and Central Africa (Kenya, Tanzania), South America (Brazil, Venezuela), Australia,
and parts of India.
The climate of savannas is marked by two distinct seasons:
•  A wet season (about 4–6 months) with heavy rainfall that allows plants to grow.
•  A dry season (about 6–8 months) when little or no rain falls, often leading to
droughts and bushﬁres.
Temperatures are high all year round, but rainfall is seasonal and unevenly distributed.
2. Adaptations of Plants and Animals
Life in the savanna requires special adaptations to survive the extreme wet–dry cycle.
Plants:
•  Trees like acacia and baobab have thick bark and deep roots to store water.
•  Grasses grow quickly in the wet season but become brown and dormant during
the dry season to conserve water.
•  Many plants have ﬁre-resistant features to survive frequent bushﬁres.
Animals:
•  Large herbivores like elephants, zebras, and giraHes migrate to ﬁnd water and
fresh grass.
•  Carnivores like lions and cheetahs adapt their hunting to follow herds.
•  Smaller animals (e.g., termites, burrowing rodents) live underground to escape
heat and predators.
3. Ecological Importance, Threats, and Conservation
Savannas are among the most biodiverse ecosystems on Earth, supporting thousands
of plant and animal species. They are also important for:
•  Carbon storage through grasslands and trees.
•  Tourism (e.g., African safaris).
•
Indigenous communities, who rely on grasslands for grazing, food, and shelter.
However, savannas face serious threats:
•  Deforestation for farming and cattle ranching.
•  Overgrazing, which damages soil and vegetation.
•  Climate change, leading to longer dry seasons and more frequent droughts.
•  Poaching and loss of wildlife habitats.
Conservation strategies include:
•  Establishing protected areas and national parks.
•  Promoting sustainable grazing and farming practices.
•  Reforestation and ﬁre management programs.
•  Educating communities and supporting eco-tourism.
