Location and Climate
Temperate grasslands are large open areas covered mainly by grasses, with very few
trees. They are found in di:erent parts of the world:
•  Prairies – North America
•  Pampas – South America
•  Steppes – Europe and Central Asia
•  Veld – South Africa
The climate of temperate grasslands is usually moderate, with hot summers and cold
winters. Rainfall is low to moderate (25–75 cm per year), which is not enough to support
large forests but good for grass growth. The strong winds and seasonal droughts make
grasses the most suitable vegetation.
2. Adaptations of Plants, Animals, and Indigenous Communities
•  Plants: Grasses grow tall in wetter areas and remain short in drier areas. They
have deep roots to absorb water and survive ﬁres or droughts.
•  Animals: Large herds of grazing animals like bison (in North America) or
antelopes (in Africa) feed on grasses. Predators like wolves and lions survive by
hunting them. Animals often migrate or burrow to escape harsh weather.
•
Indigenous Communities: People living in temperate grasslands have adapted
by practicing pastoralism (herding animals) and farming (wheat, maize, barley).
Their lifestyle often changes with the seasons, depending on water and grazing
availability.
3. Economic Importance, Threats, and Conservation
•  Economic Importance: Grasslands are known as the "breadbaskets of the
world" because they produce large amounts of wheat and maize. They also
support cattle and sheep farming, which provide meat, milk, wool, and leather.
•  Threats: Overgrazing, intensive farming, use of pesticides, and land being
converted into towns or industries are major threats. Climate change also
increases droughts and reduces soil fertility.
•  Conservation Measures:
o  Protecting grasslands as national parks and reserves
o  Practicing sustainable farming and grazing
o  Replanting native grasses and restoring ecosystems
o  Raising awareness about the importance of grasslands
