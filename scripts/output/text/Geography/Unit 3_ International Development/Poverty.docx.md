Poverty: Causes and Impacts
Source : https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/poverty.asp
1. What is Poverty?
Poverty is the condition where people lack the financial resources and essentials to maintain a minimum standard of living. It leads to hunger, poor health, lack of education, and limited opportunities for economic and social advancement.
There are two main types of poverty:
Absolute Poverty: When people cannot afford basic necessities like food, clean water, shelter, and healthcare. The World Bank defines extreme poverty as living on less than $2.15 per day.
Relative Poverty: When people earn significantly less than the average income in their country, leading to lower living standards and social exclusion.
2. Causes of Poverty
Poverty arises from multiple factors, including economic, social, political, and environmental conditions.
A. Economic Causes
Unemployment & Underemployment – Lack of jobs or low-paying jobs prevents individuals from earning a stable income.
Low Wages & Inequality – In many countries, the gap between the rich and poor continues to widen due to unequal distribution of wealth.
Economic Recessions – Financial crises lead to job losses, inflation, and reduced investment in social welfare.
Lack of Industrial Development – Some economies rely on agriculture or informal sectors, which are unstable and offer low wages.
Inflation & Rising Costs – The rising cost of living makes it difficult for low-income families to afford basic necessities.
B. Social Causes
Lack of Education – Poor education limits job opportunities, creating a cycle of poverty across generations.
Overpopulation – Rapid population growth increases pressure on resources, leading to higher unemployment and food shortages.
Health Problems & Diseases – Poor access to healthcare leads to high medical expenses, reducing disposable income.
Discrimination & Social Exclusion – Marginalized groups (women, ethnic minorities, disabled individuals) often face economic disadvantages.
C. Political Causes
Corruption & Mismanagement – Governments failing to distribute wealth fairly result in poor infrastructure, low wages, and lack of welfare programs.
Wars & Conflicts – Displaced populations lose homes, jobs, and access to education and healthcare.
Weak Governance & Unstable Policies – Ineffective social protection policies fail to address poverty issues.
D. Environmental Causes
Climate Change & Natural Disasters – Droughts, floods, and hurricanes destroy homes and agricultural land, pushing people into poverty.
Deforestation & Land Degradation – The destruction of forests and soil depletion affects rural communities dependent on farming.
Lack of Clean Water & Sanitation – Poor sanitation leads to diseases, affecting productivity and health.
3. Impacts of Poverty
Poverty affects individuals and societies in multiple ways:
A. Social Impacts
Hunger & Malnutrition – Poor nutrition leads to stunted growth, poor brain development, and weakened immunity.
Lower Life Expectancy – Poor healthcare access results in higher infant mortality and shorter lifespans.
Crime & Violence – Poverty drives people toward illegal activities like theft, drug trafficking, and human trafficking.
Education Gap – Many children from poor families drop out of school to work and support their families.
B. Economic Impacts
Low Productivity – Poor health and lack of education reduce the efficiency of workers, slowing down economic growth.
Reduced Consumer Spending – Low-income populations cannot afford goods and services, leading to slower business growth.
Increased Government Debt – Governments spend more on welfare programs and healthcare but may not have enough tax revenue.
C. Political Impacts
Social Unrest & Political Instability – Poverty leads to protests, civil wars, and political instability.
Lack of Political Participation – Poor people often have less access to voting and decision-making processes.
D. Environmental Impacts
Slum Expansion – Overcrowding in urban slums leads to poor sanitation and higher disease rates.
Deforestation & Overfarming – To survive, poor communities exploit natural resources unsustainably.
Increased Waste & Pollution – Many poverty-stricken areas lack proper waste management, leading to pollution.
4. Discrimination and Poverty
Certain groups face higher poverty risks due to discrimination:
Women: Often paid less than men and denied education or job opportunities.
Ethnic Minorities & Indigenous Communities: Excluded from economic and social benefits.
Disabled Individuals: Struggle to find employment and access basic services.
Refugees & Migrants: Face difficulties in securing jobs, housing, and healthcare.
Discrimination limits access to education, healthcare, and financial opportunities, trapping marginalized groups in a cycle of poverty.
5. How is Poverty Measured?
Several indicators measure poverty levels in a country:
GDP Per Capita – The total economic output divided by the population; higher GDP per capita suggests lower poverty.
Human Development Index (HDI) – Measures education, life expectancy, and income levels.
Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) – Examines health, education, and living standards.
Gini Coefficient – Measures income inequality; the closer to 1, the greater the inequality.
Poverty Line – The minimum income required to afford basic needs (World Bank’s benchmark is $2.15/day).
6. How to Reduce Poverty?
A. Economic Strategies
Job Creation & Fair Wages – Governments and businesses must create employment opportunities with fair wages.
Microfinance & Entrepreneurship – Providing small loans can help poor individuals start businesses.
B. Social Strategies
Quality Education for All – Free and accessible education improves future employment chances.
Healthcare Access – Affordable medical services prevent financial crises due to medical expenses.
Women Empowerment – Equal pay, property rights, and education opportunities improve economic independence.
C. Political Strategies
Anti-Corruption Measures – Ensuring transparent governance helps fair wealth distribution.
Social Welfare Programs – Subsidies for food, housing, and healthcare can support low-income groups.
D. Environmental Strategies
Sustainable Agriculture – Improved farming techniques increase food production and income.
Disaster Preparedness & Climate Resilience – Protecting vulnerable communities from climate shocks can prevent sudden poverty.
Poverty is a complex, multi-dimensional issue that affects social, economic, political, and environmental aspects of life. Reducing poverty requires a combination of policies, education, healthcare access, fair economic opportunities, and environmental sustainability.
