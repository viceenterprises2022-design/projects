Types of aid
Aid is assistance given from one country to another. It includes money, equipment,
training and loans. It can be foreign aid from the government of one country to
another - or from non-governmental organisations (NGOs) to a country or region.
There are several different types of aid:
•  Bilateral	aid (also known as 'tied aid') - the country receiving the aid must
spend the money on goods and services from the country providing it.
•  Multilateral	aid - high-income countries donate money through organisations
such as the United Nations (UN) and the World Bank.
•  Short-term emergency	aid - provides immediate support during or after a
disaster such as famine or a tsunami. It includes food, medicines and tents.
•  Long-term	aid - such as education for young people, is a continuous
programme which aims to improve standards of living.
•  Debt	abolition - is when richer countries cancel the debt owed to them by
poorer countries.
•  Non-governmental	aid - aid provided via charities. This type of aid may
provide emergency relief or may support small-scale projects such as wells to
provide clean water.
