1. Dharavi, Mumbai, India
Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aJrQPcv-daQ
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rU4V46zXrQM
https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/travel/destinations/mumbais-dharavi-whats-life-

https://realitytoursandtravel.com/city-tour/dharavi-tour-2/
Overview:
•  Location: Mumbai, Maharashtra, India
•  Population: ~1 million people
•  Size: 2.1 sq km
•  Density: ~400,000 people per sq km
•  Established: During British rule (late 19th century)
•  Major Industries: Leather, pottery, textiles, recycling
Key Issues:
•  Extreme overcrowding: Houses are often smaller than 10 sq meters, with 5-10
people living in a single room.
•  Lack of sanitation: 1 toilet per 1,440 residents.
•  High disease rates: Poor water quality leads to cholera, typhoid, and tuberculosis
outbreaks.
•  Home to over 15,000 single-room factories.
•  Generates over $1 billion annually through informal businesses.
•  Residents lack legal property rights, making them vulnerable to eviction.
•  Severe pollution due to industrial waste dumping.
•  No waste management system, causing large garbage buildup.
•  Toxic fumes from small-scale factories impact air quality.
•  Government plans "Slum Redevelopment Projects" (SRP) but residents fear
displacement.
•  Conflicts over land ownership: Developers push for high-rise apartments, while
slum dwellers demand in-situ (on-site) redevelopment.
Government Response & Solutions:
•  Mumbai Slum Rehabilitation Authority (SRA): Plans to redevelop Dharavi into
high-rise buildings with better infrastructure.
•  Opposition from residents: Many prefer to upgrade existing homes rather than
relocate to smaller apartments.
•  NGO involvement: Organizations like SPARC and Mahila Milan work to improve
living conditions and sanitation.
2. Orangi Town, Karachi, Pakistan
Sources: https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/city-and-architecture/a12232-orangi-
town-inside-one-of-the-biggest-slums-in-pakistan/
https://borgenproject.org/orangi-town-the-worlds-largest-slum/
Overview:
•  Location: Karachi, Sindh, Pakistan
•  Population: ~2.4 million (largest slum in Pakistan)
•  Size: 57 sq km (larger than Dharavi but less dense)
•  Major Industries: Home-based textile production, waste collection, small-scale
businesses
Key Issues:
•  No government services (sewage, water, electricity).
•  High illiteracy rates (~40% of children are out of school).
•  Crime and gang violence are major concerns.
•  Self-sustaining economy: Residents built their own roads, water systems, and
schools without government support.
•  Home to thousands of informal businesses, including textile workshops and small
factories.
•  Sewage problems: No proper sewage system, leading to contaminated drinking
water.
•
Illegal dumping: Waste accumulates in empty spaces, worsening health conditions.
•  Flooding risk: Karachi’s monsoons cause frequent waterlogging.
•  Government neglects the area, providing no infrastructure.
•  Orangi Pilot Project (OPP): A community-led initiative built a sewerage system
for over 90% of homes.
•  Political conflicts hinder large-scale development efforts.
Government Response & Solutions:
•  Orangi Pilot Project (OPP):
o  Developed a community-financed sewer system (one of the world’s largest).
o  Encouraged self-help housing improvements.
•  Challenges:
o  Limited government involvement.
o  Gang violence and land disputes prevent further development.
3.Tondo Slum, Manila, Philippines
Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slums_in_Metro_Manila
https://medium.com/@andystau</traveling-the-philippines-exploring-manilas-poorest-slum-
c02fdfe33a3c
Overview:
•  Location: Tondo district, Manila, Philippines
•  Population: ~4 million people in Metro Manila’s slums
•  Key Areas: Smokey Mountain (former garbage dump slum)
•  Major Industries: Informal recycling, street vending, construction
Key Issues:
•  Living in dumpsites: Many families survive by collecting and selling recyclable
materials.
•  Poor healthcare and sanitation: Dengue, cholera, and respiratory diseases are
common.
•  Child labor: Many children work instead of attending school.
•  "Garbage economy": Many slum dwellers work as waste pickers.
•
Informal jobs in construction, domestic work, and street vending dominate.
•  Lack of permanent jobs makes families financially unstable.
•  Flooding risks: Poor drainage and low-lying land lead to frequent typhoons and
floods.
•  Severe air and water pollution from unregulated industries and waste burning.
•  Forced evictions: Government demolishes slums without providing adequate
resettlement options.
•  Housing projects fail due to corruption and poor planning.
Government Response & Solutions:
•  "Smokey Mountain Resettlement Project" relocated thousands of families but
faced issues of poor construction and lack of jobs.
•  NGOs like Gawad Kalinga provide low-cost housing solutions.

Overview:
•  Location: Near the Ciliwung River, Jakarta, Indonesia
•  Population: ~1.5 million
•  Key Issue: Severe flooding destroys homes every year
•  Major Industries: Informal trading, labor work, street vending
Key Issues:
•  Poor sanitation and healthcare.
•  Unstable housing: Most houses are built on illegal land near riverbanks.
•  Most residents work in low-income informal jobs (factory workers, domestic
helpers, market vendors).
•  Lack of employment opportunities keeps families in poverty.
•  High flood risk: Located along Jakarta’s most flood-prone river.
•  Pollution: Waste dumping into the river worsens flooding.
•  Government evicts slum dwellers in the name of flood control.
•  River Normalization Project: Relocating families to government housing.
Government Response & Solutions:
•  Relocation to public housing: Government builds low-cost apartments for slum
dwellers.
•  Jakarta Smart City Plan: Focuses on slum upgrading rather than eviction.
