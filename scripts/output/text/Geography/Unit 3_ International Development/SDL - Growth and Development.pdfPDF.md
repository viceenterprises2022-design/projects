The Story of Two Growing Cities
Riya loved looking at maps. One day, her teacher showed her something unusual—two
cities that looked very similar on paper but felt completely di=erent in real life.
“Today,” the teacher said, “we are going to visit City A and City B. Both are growing… but
only one is truly developing.”
City A: The Fast-Growing City
As Riya stepped into City A, she noticed tall factories, busy roads, and trucks carrying
goods everywhere.
“Wow! This place is rich!” she said.
The guide nodded. “Yes, City A produces more and more goods every year. Its income is
rising, and its GDP is increasing.”
But as Riya walked further, she saw something else.
Some children were not in school.
Hospitals were crowded.
Many people lived in small, crowded homes.
Riya frowned. “If the city is earning so much, why aren’t people living better?”
The guide replied quietly,
“This is economic growth—the city is producing more, but the beneﬁts are not reaching
everyone.”
City B: The Developing City
Next, Riya visited City B.
It also had factories and businesses. But this city felt di=erent.
There were clean roads, parks, and schools ﬁlled with children.
Hospitals were well-equipped.
People seemed healthier and happier.
“What’s di=erent here?” Riya asked.
The guide smiled.
“City B is not just growing—it is developing. The money earned is used to improve
people’s lives.”
Riya noticed that even small details mattered:
•  Clean drinking water
•  Equal opportunities for boys and girls
•  Better jobs for people
“This is economic development,” the guide explained.
“It focuses on quality of life, not just income.”
Riya’s Big Realisation
On her way back, Riya thought deeply.
“So… both cities are earning money,” she said,
“but only one is actually improving people’s lives.”
Her teacher nodded.
“That’s the key di=erence:
•  Economic growth means the country becomes richer.
•  Economic development means people live better lives.”
Looking Beyond Numbers
Riya had one more question.
“How do we know if a country is truly developing?”
Her teacher explained:
“If we only look at GDP or income, we see growth.
But if we look at things like:
•  How long people live
•  How many children go to school
•  Whether people have equal opportunities
Then we understand development.”
“These are called development indicators,” she added.
What Helps a Country Grow and Develop?
Riya imagined what would make City A become like City B.
Her teacher explained:
•  Education helps people get better jobs
•  Healthcare keeps people strong and productive
•  Technology and infrastructure improve production
•  Good governance ensures fair distribution
“When all these come together,” she said,
“a country grows and develops.”
Final Thought
That evening, Riya wrote in her notebook:
“A country can grow without developing,
but true success is when growth improves everyone’s life.”
