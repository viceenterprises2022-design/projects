What is Free Trade?
Free trade is a way of exchanging goods and services between countries without taxes or government rules. It is meant to make it easier for countries to trade with each other and get what they need. However, there are both benefits and challenges to this system.
How Free Trade Works:
Countries focus on producing things they are good at making, then sell those goods to other countries.
In return, they buy things from other countries that they cannot produce as easily.
This improves the wealth of everyone involved, but some groups or countries might lose out.

Impact on the Environment and Society:
Free trade can also have an impact on the environment, as countries with fewer rules may cause pollution or damage ecosystems. Deforestation, for example, happens when forests are cut down to grow crops for trade.
Current Events and Free Trade:
Recently, global agreements like the Trans-Pacific Partnership (TPP) were created to increase trade between several countries, including the United States. However, many countries debated whether these agreements were fair. Some leaders, like Donald Trump, believed that free trade was hurting jobs in their country, so they worked on changing trade policies, such as renegotiating NAFTA (the North American Free Trade Agreement).
In Summary:
Free trade is a system where countries exchange goods and services with little to no government control. While it can bring benefits like cheaper products and more jobs, it can also hurt small businesses, harm the environment, and cause job losses in certain industries.
