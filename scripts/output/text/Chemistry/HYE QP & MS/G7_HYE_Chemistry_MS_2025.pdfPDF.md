Criteria B
4 i) RQ“How can we separate a mixture of water, iron filings, sand, and salt into four pure
substances using physical properties (magnetism, solubility, particle size, and boiling
point)?”
ii) Variables: IV: Type of separation technique
DV: Physical properties of each component
CV: Temperature, amount of each component taken, volume of distilled water (any 2)
iii) Materials and apparatus:
Beakers (250 mL ×2), stirring rod, strong magnet (wrapped in cling film or a zip bag), filter
funnel, filter paper (e.g., Whatman No. 1), retort stand, evaporating basin/watch glass, hot
plate (preferred) or Bunsen + tripod + gauze, tongs, balance (±0.01 g), measuring cylinder
(100 mL), distillation kit (optional: condenser, flask, delivery tube, thermometer, receiver),
safety goggles, heat-proof mat, gloves, waste container.
Procedure:
Step 1: Get the iron out
Wrap a magnet in a plastic bag or plastic wrap. Hold the magnet near the mixture and move it
around to pull all the magnetic iron filings out. Once collected, you can peel off the bag to
remove the iron easily.
Step 2: Add water to the rest
Pour the remaining mixture of sand and salt into a beaker. Add water and stir until all the salt
disappears. The salt will dissolve in the water, but the sand will not.
Step 3: Filter out the sand
Set up a filter funnel with filter paper over an empty, clean beaker. Slowly pour the sand and
saltwater mixture into the filter. The filter paper will catch the sand, and the saltwater will
pass through into the beaker below.
Step 4: Dry the sand
Carefully take the filter paper with the wet sand out of the funnel. Put it on a plate or dish and
let it dry. You can use a hot plate on low heat or a warm oven to speed up the process.
Step 5: Evaporate the water
Pour the saltwater solution from the beaker into an evaporating dish. Gently heat it over a low
flame. The water will evaporate away as steam, leaving behind the salt. Stop heating once the
crystals start to form.
Step 6: Let the salt dry
Allow the dish with the salt crystals to cool completely. The dried salt is now separated and
can be weighed.
Safety Measures:
Heat: Use hot plate where possible; handle hot glass with tongs; heat-proof mat.
Glassware: Check for cracks; carry carefully.
Iron filings: Keep away from eyes; use magnet wrapped in plastic to avoid
loss/contamination

Criterion D
6. i)
•
Isotopes enable non-invasive medical diagnosis, such as Technetium-99m
imaging.
•  They assist archaeologists in accurately dating artefacts using Carbon-14.
•  Environmental scientists use isotopic signatures to trace pollution pathways.
•  Radioactive isotopes generate long-lasting waste that poses contamination risks.
•  The safe production and disposal of isotopes require strict regulation.
•  Workers and nearby communities must be protected from radiation exposure.
•  Regions with weak healthcare systems may face higher health risks due to poor
monitoring.
•  While isotopes provide significant scientific benefits, they demand careful
management to reduce environmental and ethical concerns.
ii) Advantages
1.  Radioactive isotopes enable highly accurate medical imaging, improving diagnosis
and treatment.
2.  They allow precise archaeological dating, helping researchers study ancient
artefacts.
3.  They support environmental monitoring, such as tracing pollution and studying
ecosystems.
Disadvantages
1.  Radioactive isotopes generate hazardous waste that requires long-term safe storage.
2.  They pose risks of environmental contamination if not managed properly.
3.  Their use demands high-cost safety systems and regulations, which may not be
feasible everywhere.
iii) •  Understanding isotopes helps students learn how radiation works in real-world
contexts.
•  It allows students to appreciate why hospitals follow strict radiation safety rules.
•  Students undergoing medical scans can understand why Technetium-99m is used for
imaging.
•  Knowledge of controlled exposure helps students make safer personal health decisions.
•  Learning how isotopes trace pollution encourages support for environmental protection
policies.
•  This understanding can inspire students to pursue careers in nuclear medicine or
environmental science.
