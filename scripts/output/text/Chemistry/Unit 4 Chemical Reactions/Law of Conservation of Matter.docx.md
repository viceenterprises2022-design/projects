Law of Conservation of Matter
What is the Law of Conservation of Matter?
The Law of Conservation of Matter states that:
Matter cannot be created or destroyed during a physical or chemical change.
This means:
The total mass before a reaction is equal to the total mass after the reaction
atoms are rearranged, but they do not disappear
Matter and Atoms
Everything around us is made of matter.
Matter is made up of tiny particles called atoms.
During changes:
atoms may separate
atoms may join together differently
atoms are never lost
Physical Changes and Conservation of Matter
A physical change changes only the form or state of matter.
Examples:
melting ice
boiling water
tearing paper
The particles remain the same substance.
Example: Melting Ice
Ice changes into liquid water.
The substance remains water, so matter is conserved.
Chemical Changes and Conservation of Matter
A chemical change forms new substances.
Examples:
rusting iron
burning wood
cooking food
Atoms rearrange to form new compounds.
Example of a Chemical Reaction
Hydrogen reacts with oxygen to form water.
Count the atoms:
The number of atoms is equal on both sides.
Why Chemical Equations Must Be Balanced
Balanced equations show that:
matter is conserved
atoms are neither created nor destroyed
reactions follow scientific laws correctly
Everyday Examples of Conservation of Matter
Burning a Candle
The wax changes into gases like carbon dioxide and water vapor.
Matter is not lost; it changes form.
Rusting Iron
Iron reacts with oxygen from the air to form rust.
The atoms are rearranged into a new substance called iron oxide.
Key Vocabulary
Important Points to Remember
Matter cannot disappear.
Atoms rearrange during reactions.
Balanced equations follow the law of conservation of matter.
Mass remains constant in a closed system.

A large iron bridge near a coastal city was exposed to moist air and rainwater for many years. Engineers noticed reddish-brown rust forming on the surface of the bridge. Rust forms when iron reacts with oxygen and water in the environment.

During this process:
iron atoms combine with oxygen atoms
no atoms are destroyed
the total mass of matter remains conserved

Engineers painted the bridge with protective coatings to reduce rusting and increase safety.
Real-Life Importance
Understanding conservation of matter helps scientists and engineers:
design safer buildings and bridges
reduce corrosion damage
calculate amounts of chemicals in industries
protect materials from environmental changes
