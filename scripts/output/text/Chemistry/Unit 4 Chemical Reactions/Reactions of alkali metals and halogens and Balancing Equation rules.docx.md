Step-wise method to balance a chemical equation
Example (unbalanced): H₂ + O₂ → H₂O
Step 1: Write the correct formula
Make sure each substance is written correctly:
Hydrogen = H₂, Oxygen = O₂, Water = H₂O.
Step 2: Count atoms on both sides
Oxygen is not balanced.
Step 3: Balance the element that is not balanced (oxygen)
To make oxygen equal, put 2 in front of water:
H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Now count again:
Oxygen: Left 2, Right 2 ✅
Hydrogen: Left 2, Right 4 ❌ (not balanced now)
Step 4: Balance hydrogen
Put 2 in front of H₂:
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Step 5: Check again (final check)
✅ Balanced equation: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Key rules students must remember
Never change the small subscripts in a formula (e.g., do not change H₂O to H₂O₂).
Only add coefficients in front (numbers like 2, 3, 4).
Balance one element at a time, then recheck at the end.
1. Reactions of Alkali Metals with Water
Alkali metals (such as lithium, sodium, and potassium) are highly reactive.
They react with water to form:
A metal hydroxide
Hydrogen gas
Heat energy (exothermic reaction)
Metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen
Example: Sodium reacts vigorously with water. Hydrogen gas is released, and the solution becomes alkaline due to sodium hydroxide.
Word equation: Sodium + Water → Sodium hydroxide + Hydrogen
Balanced equation: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂
2. Reactions of Alkali Metals with Oxygen
Alkali metals also react with oxygen in air to form metal oxides.
Example: Lithium combines with oxygen to form lithium oxide. This is a synthesis (combination) reaction.
Word equation:Lithium + Oxygen → Lithium oxide
Balanced equation: 4Li + O₂ → 2Li₂O
3. Reactions of Halogens with Metals
Halogens (such as chlorine and bromine) react with metals to form metal halides.
These reactions are usually fast and form ionic compounds.
Example: Sodium loses an electron and chlorine gains an electron, forming the compound sodium chloride (common salt).
Word equation: Sodium + Chlorine → Sodium chloride
Balanced equation: 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl
4. Reactions of Halogens with Hydrogen
Halogens also react with hydrogen gas to form hydrogen halides.
Example: Hydrogen and chlorine combine to form hydrogen chloride gas. When dissolved in water, it forms hydrochloric acid.
Word equation: Hydrogen + Chlorine → Hydrogen chloride
Balanced equation: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl
