What is Valency?
Valency is the combining capacity of an atom — the number of electrons it can donate, accept, or share
to achieve a stable electron configuration (usually a full outer shell of 8 electrons, called the octet rule).

The Crisscross Method — Step by Step
Rules:
1.  Write symbols of cation first, anion second
2.  Write the valency of each as a small
superscript
3.  Swap the valencies — cation's valency
becomes anion's subscript and vice versa
4.  Simplify the subscripts to lowest ratio if
possible
5.  If a polyatomic ion gets a subscript > 1,
enclose it in brackets
Common Mistakes to Avoid:
•  Do NOT simplify MgO to Mg₁O₁ — it stays
MgO
•  Ca²⁺ + O²⁻ → Ca₂O₂ → simplify → CaO  ✓
•  Al³⁺ + O²⁻ → Al₂O₃  (no simplification since
2 and 3 have no common factor)
•  Na⁺ + SO₄²⁻ → Na₂SO₄  (not Na₂(SO₄)₁)
•  Ca²⁺ + NO₃⁻ → Ca(NO₃)₂  (brackets
needed!)

PART 2: BALANCING EQUATIONS
Why Balance Equations?
The Law of Conservation of Mass states that
matter cannot be created or destroyed. In any
chemical reaction, the total mass of reactants
equals the total mass of products. Balancing
ensures the same number of each type of atom
appears on both sides.
The 4-Step Balancing Method:
6.  Write the unbalanced (skeleton) equation
7.  Make an atom count table for each element
8.  Add coefficients to balance — start with
metals, then non-metals, H and O last
9.  Double-check all atom counts match on
both sides
Key Vocabulary:
•  Reactants — substances on the LEFT of
the arrow
•  Products — substances on the RIGHT of
the arrow
•  Coefficient — number placed in FRONT of
a formula
•  Subscript — small number WITHIN a
formula (never change this!)
Remember:
•  Coefficients multiply ALL atoms in the
formula
•  Never alter subscripts inside a formula
•  A coefficient of 1 is invisible (not written)

The reactivity series ranks metals in order of how readily they react — i.e., how easily they lose
electrons and form positive ions. More reactive metals react faster, more vigorously, and with weaker
reagents.
