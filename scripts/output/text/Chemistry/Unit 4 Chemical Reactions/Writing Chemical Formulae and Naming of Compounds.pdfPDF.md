Writing Chemical Formulae and Naming of Compounds
Lesson Goals and ATL Skills
Master writing correct chemical formulae using valency (combining power), polyatomic
(group) ions, and Roman numerals for variable charge metals. This builds critical thinking
by  applying  the  "cross  method"  to  ensure  electrical  neutrality  (total  positive  charge
equals total negative charge).
Part 1: Formulae from Compound Names
Compound names reveal elements and atom ratios. Covalent compounds use Greek
preﬁxes; ionic compounds use valency for charge balance (no preﬁxes).
Covalent Examples:
•  Carbon monoxide: mono- (1 O) → CO
•  Carbon dioxide: di- (2 O) → CO₂
•  Nitrogen trichloride: tri- (3 Cl) → NCl₃
•  Dinitrogen tetroxide: di- (2 N), tetra- (4 O) → N₂O₄
•  Dinitrogen pentoxide: di- (2 N), penta- (5 O) → N₂O₅
Ionic Examples:
•  Sodium chloride: Na⁺ (1), Cl⁻ (1) → NaCl
•  Magnesium bromide: Mg²⁺ (2), Br⁻ (1) → MgBr₂
•  Potassium oxide: K⁺ (1), O²⁻ (2) → K₂O
Pitfalls: Confusing preﬁxes (e.g., CO₂ as monoxide); ignoring charge balance in ionic
(e.g., MgBr).

Cross Method Steps:
1.  Write symbols with valencies (ignore signs initially).
2.  Cross valencies as subscripts.
3.  Simplify ratios by dividing by the greatest common divisor.
4.  Drop 1's; metal ﬁrst.
Examples:
•  Sodium + Oxygen: Na (1) × O (2) → Na₂O
•  Calcium + Chlorine: Ca (2) × Cl (1) → CaCl₂
•  Aluminium + Nitrogen: Al (3) × N (3) → AlN (simplify 3:3)
•  Magnesium + Oxygen: Mg (2) × O (2) → Mg₂O₂ → MgO
Pitfalls: Forgetting to simplify (Mg₂O₂); adding charges in ﬁnal formula.

Rules: Brackets () for 2+ group ions; the cross method applies.
Examples:
•  Calcium hydroxide: Ca²⁺ × (OH⁻)¹ → Ca(OH)₂
•  Sodium nitrate: Na⁺ × (NO₃⁻)¹ → NaNO₃ (no bracket)
•  Aluminium sulfate: Al³⁺ × (SO₄²⁻) → Al₂(SO₄)₃
•  Ammonium carbonate: (NH₄⁺)¹ × (CO₃²⁻) → (NH₄)₂CO₃
Pitfalls: No brackets (CaOH₂); fusing ions (Al₂SO₄₃); ignoring multiples.

Key Rules Summary
•  Total charge = 0 (drives all formulae).
•  Metal (cation) ﬁrst, then non-metal/anion.
•  Subscripts for atoms; brackets for polyatomics >1.
•  Simplify ratios; Roman numerals for variable metals.

Practice:
1.  Why CaCl₂, not CaCl? (Ca²⁺ needs 2 Cl⁻ for neutrality.)
2.  MgO vs. Mg(OH)₂? (O²⁻ vs. 2(OH⁻); dioerent ions.)
3.  FeO vs. Fe₂O₃? (Fe²⁺ vs. Fe³⁺ valencies.)
4.  Bracket role? (Shows multiple polyatomic units, e.g., (SO₄)₃.)
Quick Checks:
•  Na₂O correct (not NaO): 2 Na⁺ balance O²⁻.
•  Brackets in Ca(OH)₂ (not NaNO₃): Multiples vs. single.
(III) in Fe₂O₃: Fe³⁺ charge.
•
•  MgO from Mg₂O₂: Simplify 2:2 ratio.
Study Tips: Practice 20 formulae daily using cross method; quiz on polyatomics; explain
charge balance aloud. Focus on patterns over rote memorization for deep understanding.
