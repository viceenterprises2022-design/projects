Criterion D – Reflecting on the Impacts of Science
1.  Pure substances (elements and compounds) and mixtures are used differently in
everyday life. For example:
Pure elements like copper are excellent conductors and are used in wires.
Compounds like sodium chloride are essential for food, while water (H₂O) is vital
for life.
Mixtures like alloys (e.g., steel, brass) combine useful properties of elements.
Air, a mixture of gases, is essential for breathing and combustion.
Scientists, industry, and society make decisions about whether to use pure substances or
mixtures, depending on cost, availability, performance, health, and environmental
impact.

2. Scenario: River Water Crisis in a Village
A small village near a river depends on it for drinking, cooking, and farming. After heavy
rainfall, the river water becomes muddy and unsafe. Scientists tested samples and found:
•  Suspended clay and silt particles make water turbid.
•  Organic matter and microbes are present in the water.
•  Farmers nearby also use chemical dyes in textile processing, which sometimes
seep into the river.
The community has limited resources. They can use simple methods like sedimentation,
decantation, filtration, boiling, and sometimes solar distillation. A local NGO also
introduces a low-cost chromatography kit to check for harmful dyes in water.
a.  Applying your knowledge of separation techniques (sedimentation, filtration,
distillation, chromatography) can help villagers make the water safe. Discuss
Which technique(s) would be most practical and why?
b.  Evaluate the benefits and limitations of using simple methods (like sedimentation
and filtration) compared to advanced methods (like distillation or lab
chromatography).
c.  Discuss whether industries near the river should be allowed to continue dyeing
fabrics if their waste pollutes water sources. Justify your answer.
