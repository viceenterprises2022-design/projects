Atomic Number(Z): The number of electrons or protons in an atom is called
the atomic number of that atom. It is represented by the letter Z
EX: In a helium atom, there are two protons in the nucleus and two electrons
revolving in the orbit around the nucleus. So, the atomic number (z) of helium is
2.
Mass Number(A): It is equal to the sum of the number of protons (p) and
number of neutrons (n) in the nucleus.
Mass number = Number of Protons + Number of Neutrons.
EX: In a sodium atom, there are 11 Protons and 12 neutrons. Hence, its mass
number (A) is 23 (11 + 12).
Therefore, number of neutrons (n) = Mass Number (A) – Number of protons (Z)
n = A – Z

Atomic Models:
DALTON’S THEORY OF ATOM: (Billiard Ball model)
J. Dalton developed his famous theory of atom in 1803. The main postulates of
•  Atom was considered as a smallest, dense and hard indivisible particle.
•  Each element consists of a specific type of atoms.
•  The properties of elements differ because of difference in kinds of atom
contained in them.
•  This theory provides a satisfactory basis for law of chemical combination.
Limitations of Dalton’s Theory :
•  Dalton’s theory fails to explain why atoms of different kinds should
differ in valency and mass etc.
•  The discovery of isotopes showed that atoms of same elements have
different atomic masses
•  The discovery of various sub-aomic particles like protons, electrons, X-
rays etc. during late 19th century lead to idea that the atom was no longer
an indivisible and smallest particle of the matter.
Thomson’s Model :
•  Thomson was the first proposed a detailed model of atom.
•  Thomson proposed that an atom consist of a uniform sphere of
positive (+ve) charge in which electrons are present at some places.
•  This model of atom is known as ‘Plum Pudding model’.
Limitations of Thomson’s Theory
•  An important drawback of this model is that the mas of the atoms
is considered to be evenly spread over that atom.
•  It is a static model. It does not reflect the movement of electron.
Rutherford’s a-Scattering Experiment
y Rutherford carried out a-particles scattering experiment by the bombardment
of high speed a-particle on thin foil of gold, emitted from radius and gave the
following observations, which was based on his experiment.
y The angular deflections of scattered a-particles were studied with the help of
moving microscope.
Observations
Rutherford execute a number of experiments, involving the scattering of α-
particles (He+2) by very thin foil of gold.
Observations were:
•  Most of the α-particles (99%) passes through it, without any deviation or
deflection.
•  Some of the α-particles were deflected through small angles.
•  Very few α-particles were deflected by large angles and occasionally an
α-particle got deflected by 180°.
Conclusions :
•  Most of the α-particle (He+2) went straight through metal foil undeflected,
it means that there must be very large empty space within atom.
•  Since few of the α-particles were deflected from their original path
through moderate angle; it was concluded that whole of the positive (+ve)
charge is concentrated and the space occupied by this positive charge is
very small in the atom.
•  Positively (+ve) charged heavy mass which occupies only a small volume
in an atom is known as nucleus. Nucleus is supposed to be present at the
centre of atom.
Bohr’s Model of Atom:
Electronic configuration
•  We can represent the structure of the atom in two ways: using diagrams
called electron shell diagrams or by writing out a special notation called
the electronic configuration (or electronic structure or electron
distribution)
Electron shell diagrams
•  Electrons orbit the nucleus in shells (or energy levels) and each shell has
a different amount of energy associated with it
•  The further away from the nucleus, the more energy a shell has
•  Electrons fill the shell closest to the nucleus
•  When a shell becomes full of electrons, additional electrons have to be
added to the next shell
o  The first shell can hold 2 electrons
o  The second shell can hold 8 electrons
o  The third shell can hold 8 electrons
o  Then the fourth shell starts to fill
•  The outermost shell of an atom is called the valence shell and an atom is
much more stable if it can manage to completely fill this shell with
electrons
•  The arrangement of electrons in shells can also be explained using
•
numbers
Instead of drawing electron shell diagrams, the number of electrons in
each electron shell can be written down, separated by commas
•  This notation is called the electronic configuration (or electronic
structure)
o  E.g. Carbon has 6 electrons, 2 in the 1st shell and 4 in the 2nd shell
▪
Its electronic configuration is 2,4
Example:
Draw and write the electronic structure of magnesium.
Answer:
•  Magnesium has 12 electrons in total.
•  A maximum of two can fit in the first shell and eight in the second shell.
•  The remaining two will occupy the third shell.
•  The written form of this electronic structure is 2,8,2
