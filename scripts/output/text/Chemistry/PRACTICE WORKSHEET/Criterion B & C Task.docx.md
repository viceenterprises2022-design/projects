Criterion B Task: The Baker's Dilemma
Key Concept: Change
Related Concepts: Models, Evidence, Planning
Global Context: Scientific and technical innovation
Scenario
A baker accidentally spilled a bag of clean, dry sand into a large bucket of clear sugar water solution. The mixture now contains sand, dissolved sugar, and water. The baker needs to recover the sand and the sugar separately so they can still be used. They have asked for your help in designing a safe and efficient scientific investigation to separate these three components.
You have access to standard school laboratory equipment: beakers, funnels, filter paper, Bunsen burners, tripods, gauze mats, evaporating dishes, stirring rods, water baths, and electronic balances.
Clearly explain the scientific problem you are trying to solve in this investigation
Formulate a hypothesis using if, then , because format. Your hypothesis should suggest how well you think you can recover the original amounts of sand and sugar.
Identify the independent, dependent, and at least one controlled variable for your investigation.
Design a step-by-step procedure to separate the sand, sugar, and water. Your method must be safe, detailed, and use the provided equipment effectively. Include relevant safety considerations.
