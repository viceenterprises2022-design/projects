Atom, elements, compounds – symbols and formulae
Criteria  B  task  –  Design  an  experiment  to  show  how  two  elements  combine  to  form
compounds with observably different properties to the original elements. Students must
include aim, hypothesis, procedure (As for example, magnesium and oxygen reaction)
Aim: To investigate how the combination of magnesium and oxygen results in the formation
of magnesium oxide with different properties than the original elements.
Hypothesis:  If  the  Independent  variable  (IV),  that  is  magnesium  react  with  oxygen,  the
dependent variable (DV) that is the magnesium oxide is formed, which has different physical
and chemical properties than the original elements.
Procedure:
1.  Collect the following materials: magnesium ribbon, a Bunsen burner, a crucible and
lid, a weighing scale, a test tube, a delivery tube, and a water trough.
2.  Cut a small piece of magnesium ribbon and weigh it using the scale. Record its mass.
3.  Place the magnesium ribbon inside the crucible and cover it with the lid.
4.  Connect the delivery tube to the test tube and place the test tube in the water trough.
5.  Heat the crucible using the Bunsen burner until the magnesium ribbon reacts with the
oxygen in the air, producing magnesium oxide.
6.  Observe the test tube and record any changes in the water level, indicating the release
of gas.
7.  Allow the crucible to cool and weigh it again, along with the magnesium oxide inside.
Record the mass.
8.  Calculate the mass of the magnesium oxide formed and compare it to the initial mass
of the magnesium ribbon.
9.  Examine the properties of the magnesium oxide, such as its color, melting point, and
solubility, and compare them to the properties of magnesium and oxygen.
Observations:
•  Before the reaction: a shiny, silvery piece of magnesium ribbon
•  During the reaction: the magnesium ribbon glows brightly and a white powder forms
on its surface
•  After the reaction: a white powder (magnesium oxide) remains in the crucible
Conclusion:  The  experiment  demonstrated  that  magnesium  and  oxygen  can  react  to  form
magnesium  oxide,  which  has  different  properties  than  the  original  elements.  Magnesium
oxide is a white solid with a high melting point and is insoluble in water, whereas magnesium
is a shiny, silvery metal that is relatively soft and easily oxidized, and oxygen is a colorless,
odorless gas that supports combustion. The amount of magnesium used in the experiment
affected  the  amount  of  magnesium  oxide  produced,  but  the  properties  of  the  magnesium
oxide were consistent regardless of the amount used.
