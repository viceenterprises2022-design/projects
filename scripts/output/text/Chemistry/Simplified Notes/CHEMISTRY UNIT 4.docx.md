Unit 4: Chemical Reactions
1. Writing Chemical Formulas
What is a Chemical Formula?
A chemical formula tells us:
which elements are present
how many atoms are in the compound
Examples
Water = H₂O
Carbon dioxide = CO₂
Sodium chloride = NaCl
What is Valency?
Definition
Valency is the combining power of an atom.
It tells us:
how many electrons an atom gains, loses, or shares
Easy Way to Think About Valency
Atoms want a full outer shell.
They:
lose electrons
gain electrons
share electrons
to become stable.

2. Balancing Chemical Equations
What is a Chemical Equation?
A chemical equation shows:
reactants → products
Example
Hydrogen + oxygen → water
Law of Conservation of Mass
Matter cannot be created or destroyed.
So:
number of atoms before reaction
=
number of atoms after reaction
What are Coefficients?
Numbers placed in front of formulas.
Example:
2H₂O
means:
2 water molecules
Steps for Balancing Equations
Example
Unbalanced:
Step 1: Count atoms
Oxygen is not balanced.
Step 2: Add coefficient
Now balanced.
Important Rule
Never change small numbers inside formulas.
Correct:
2H₂O
Wrong:
H₂O₂
3. Types of Reactions
A. Synthesis (Combination) Reaction
Definition
Two or more substances combine to form one product.
General Form
A + B → AB
Example
Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide
B. Decomposition Reaction
Definition
One compound breaks into simpler substances.
General Form
AB → A + B
Example
C. Single Displacement Reaction
Definition
One element replaces another element.
General Form
A + BC → AC + B
Example
Zinc replaces copper.
D. Double Displacement Reaction
Definition
Two compounds exchange ions.
General Form
AB + CD → AD + CB
Example
E. Combustion Reaction
Definition
A substance reacts with oxygen and releases heat.
Example
Methane burns in oxygen.
